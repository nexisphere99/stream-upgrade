# DAY 2 — "The Gap" (Part 1: Morning / Ramen)

---

I wake up before the alarm.

That hasn't happened in months. Maybe longer. My body's internal clock has been set to "ignore all stimuli until forced" since approximately freshman year of college (which I attended for one semester before the financial aid fell through and I discovered that motivation is a finite resource). But today my eyes open at 9:42 AM — forty-eight minutes before the alarm I actually set last night, the one Kira told me to set, the one she specifically said should be *real* and not the kind I snooze for ninety minutes — and I'm just... awake.

The ceiling crack is there. Still growing. Still splitting the plaster like the building's skull is fracturing in slow motion. But this morning it looks less like a verdict and more like geography. Just a crack. Just a thing that exists in a room where I also exist, and today that room contains a plan.

Kira's picking me up at noon.

I lie on the mattress for three minutes, not because I'm tired but because the warmth of the comforter is the closest thing to a hug my life currently offers, and I want to be strategic about when I leave it. My phone is on the floor beside me. I pick it up. No new messages — it's early by my standards and Kira said she had morning yoga. I imagine her in some studio somewhere, a bright room with wooden floors and too many plants, bending into shapes that my spine would file a restraining order against. She probably wears one of those fitted tank tops. The kind that—

Okay. Stop. Get up.

I get up.

The apartment is the same. Obviously. Apartments don't rearrange themselves overnight based on the occupant's mood, though this one seems to try — the light from the window is hitting differently today, catching dust motes in a way that looks almost cinematic rather than depressing. Or maybe that's just me projecting. Maybe when you have somewhere to be and someone to see, the particles in the air rearrange themselves into something you can tolerate.

I step over the Monster can I left on the floor last night. The microwave burrito wrapper is still on the counter. The second monitor's standby light glows softly, the dashboard from last night's stream still queued up behind the dark screen, ready to remind me of my failures the moment I jiggle the mouse. I don't jiggle the mouse.

First things first: stream. Even today. Especially today. Because if I don't stream, the day becomes entirely about Kira, and that's too much pressure on a lunch that is not a date (except that Marcus called it a date, and she said "good boy" with a smirk emoji, and I called click-through rates hot in my head approximately forty times before falling asleep, but it's not a date).

I shower. Really shower. Not the lukewarm speed-rinse I usually do — I wait for the hot water, all eight minutes of it, standing there like a patient idiot while the pipes clank and groan and eventually produce something approaching warmth. I use the bar soap on my hair and then — and then I actually dig under the bathroom sink, past the extra toilet paper roll and the bottle of drain cleaner I bought six months ago and never used, and find a travel-size bottle of shampoo. It's from a hotel. I've never stayed in a hotel. Marcus gave it to me as a joke for my birthday — a little bottle of Marriott shampoo with a note that said "for emergencies." I think this qualifies.

The shampoo smells like coconut and something vaguely chemical. I work it into my hair with more effort than I've applied to my physical appearance in recent memory. Rinse. My hair feels... different. Lighter. Less like it's been lacquered to my skull.

I get out. Towel off. And I do something I almost never do.

I look in the mirror.

It's not a revelation. The face staring back is the same one that's always been there — pale, dark circles, acne along the jawline, thin lips, the nose with the wide bridge, the eyes that can't quite commit to making contact even with their own reflection. My hair is wet and pushed back from my forehead and without the greasy curtain falling over it, my face looks more exposed. More there. More *seen*, in a way that makes me want to pull the hair back down immediately.

But I don't. I just look.

Marcus told me to wear the grey shirt. The grey shirt is the softest of the three and also the one with the fewest visible stains. I pull it on. Same black joggers — I only own the one pair, and laundry isn't happening before noon. I look at the crushed sneakers by the door and experience a brief, futile wish for better shoes. The sneakers go on. The navy hoodie goes on over the grey shirt. I zip it halfway, which is the closest thing to a "look" I'm capable of assembling.

9:58 AM. I've got two hours before Kira arrives. Time to stream.
