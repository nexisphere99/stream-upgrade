# DAY 2 — "The Gap" (Part 3: Kira Route — The Confession)

---

I don't laugh.

I don't look away. I don't run my hand through my hair and force a chuckle and say "kidding, kidding" and let the moment dissolve into the safe formaldehyde of plausible deniability. I've done that my whole life — deflected every vulnerable thing I've ever said by wrapping it in irony before anyone could take it seriously. Every compliment downgraded to a joke. Every real feeling wrapped in three layers of "haha just kidding" until the feeling suffocated.

Not this time.

My hands are on my knees. My face is burning. Every synapse in my brain is screaming *take it back, take it back, say you were joking, laugh, deflect, survive* — and I don't. I sit there on Kira Nomura's couch in her apartment with her ring light and her $487 dashboard and I look at her and I let the word stand.

*Hot.*

I called her hot. To her face. In her apartment. While she was explaining click-through rates. This is either the bravest or the stupidest thing I've ever done, and the distance between those two options is narrower than I'd like.

Three seconds pass. They contain entire geological epochs. Continents drift. Stars form and die. I age a decade.

Kira doesn't look away either.

Her eyes are wide behind the glasses — not shocked-wide, but *open*-wide, like something she's been holding shut just cracked open and the light is pouring in. The pink on her cheeks has deepened into something genuine, a flush that starts at her jawline and rises past her cheekbones. Her hands are still clasped in front of her, knuckles slightly white. The pixel-heart tattoo pulses with the rhythm of her wrist.

<<kira "...did you just call click-through rates hot?">>

<<eli "I called you hot. The click-through rates are a bonus.">>

I say it. Out loud. With my whole chest and my whole burning face and the absolute reckless conviction of a man who has $47 in the bank and nothing left to lose. The words come out steady, which surprises me, because inside I am a building on fire watching itself burn with detached fascination.

Kira uncrosses her legs. Recrosses them. The desk chair creaks under the shift of her weight. Her mouth opens. Closes. Opens again. I have never — in a year and a half of knowing her, in hundreds of Discord calls and thousands of messages and a slowly expanding encyclopedia of her expressions and habits and tells — I have never seen Kira Nomura at a loss for words.

<<kira "Eli. You can't just say that.">>

<<eli "Why not?">>

<<kira "Because I've been not-saying it for a year and a half and you can't just waltz in here and say it first when I've been practicing.">>

The room tilts. The floor shifts. Something fundamental about the geometry of my life rearranges itself in real time and I feel it happening — the tectonic slide of everything I assumed about us grinding against everything that might actually be true.

<<eli "Practicing?">>

<<kira "In the shower. Like a normal person.">>

She says it fast. Almost too fast, like the words have been pressurized and the seal just broke. Her hands unlace and relace in front of her. She pushes her glasses up. Pushes them up again even though they haven't slipped. The nervous gesture on loop, a fidget that says everything her composure is trying to hide.

I stare at her. She stares at me.

And then I laugh.

It comes from somewhere deep and surprised, a sound I don't recognize as mine — not the dead, performative laugh I produce on stream, not the reluctant cough-laugh Marcus sometimes pulls out of me, but something new. Something involuntary. Something that sounds, for the first time in a long time, like a person who's happy.

Kira laughs too. The real laugh. The high, sudden burst she can't suppress — it breaks through the pressed lips and the composure and the wire-frame glasses and it crinkles her nose and it makes her whole face scrunch up in exactly the way I've been cataloging for eighteen months. She covers her mouth with her hand but it doesn't help. The laugh is bigger than her hand. The laugh fills the room.

<<eli "You've been practicing. In the shower.">>

<<kira "Don't make fun of me!">>

<<eli "I'm not making fun. I'm — I'm impressed. I didn't practice. I just blurted it out like an idiot.">>

<<kira "It was very smooth.">>

<<eli "'It's hot' while you're talking about thumbnails. Very smooth. Casanova energy.">>

<<kira "You know what, it kind of worked? Like, objectively terrible timing, but—">>

<<eli "But?">>

She takes her glasses off. Holds them in both hands. Without them, her face is naked — the dark brown eyes unframed, wider than they look behind the lenses, slightly shiny in the afternoon light. She looks at me the way she looked at the dashboard earlier, except warmer. More scared. More real.

<<kira "It worked.">>

Two words. The room goes quiet again, but this time the silence isn't charged with panic — it's charged with something softer, something that hums at a frequency I can feel in my sternum. The PC fans whir. The afternoon light shifts. We sit three feet apart on two different pieces of furniture and the three feet feel like both the shortest and longest distance in the world.

<<eli "We should probably talk about this.">>

<<kira "Or we could stream together and pretend it didn't happen.">>

<<eli "Or we could get dinner.">>

<<kira "...dinner works.">>

---

She doesn't put her glasses back on right away. She holds them in her lap, fidgeting with the wire frames, bending them slightly and unbending them in a rhythm that matches whatever's happening in her head. I watch her hands. The small, precise movements. The pixel-heart tattoo appearing and disappearing as her wrist rotates.

<<kira "There's an izakaya two blocks from here. It's tiny. Like, twelve seats. The lighting is terrible.">>

<<eli "I love terrible lighting.">>

<<kira "I know you do.">>

She says it with a half-smile that contains more knowledge than the words suggest. *I know you do.* I know you prefer dim rooms. I know you avoid your reflection. I know the dark is where you feel safest. She knows these things about me because she's been paying attention for a year and a half, the same way I've been paying attention to her, and we've both been building archives of each other without ever naming what the archives were for.

<<kira "Give me ten minutes to change?">>

<<eli "You look fine.">>

<<kira "I've been in this turtleneck for eight hours and I did hot yoga in it this morning.">>

<<eli "...ten minutes is fine.">>

She disappears into the bedroom. The door doesn't fully close — it stays open a crack, a sliver of light and movement visible through the gap. I don't look. I absolutely do not look. I look at my phone instead, which has three notifications: a text from Marcus, a Discord message from DarkSlayer (somehow), and a news alert about something I don't care about.

<<marc "how's the date going">>

Kira emerges from the bedroom seven minutes later. She's changed into a cream-colored oversized knit sweater that hangs off one shoulder, showing the strap of a black tank top underneath and the line of her collarbone. Different jeans — high-waisted, dark wash, the kind that make her legs look longer than they are, which at 5'3" is a geometric achievement. Same platform sneakers. She's put the glasses back on but her hair is slightly different — she's tucked one side behind her ear, which she doesn't usually do, and it changes the shape of her face in a way that makes me forget, briefly, how to breathe.

She's also wearing lip gloss. She wasn't wearing lip gloss before.

I notice this. I notice the lip gloss. I notice that she went into her bedroom to change for dinner with me and she put on lip gloss and I'm going to think about this every day for the rest of my life.

<<kira "Ready?">>

I'm doing that. She's right. My face is doing something it hasn't done in months — arranging itself into an expression that isn't fatigue or frustration or the flat neutral I wear like a mask. I'm smiling. Actually, genuinely smiling. And I can't stop.

The izakaya is called *Kitsune*. It is, as advertised, tiny — a narrow room with a wooden bar counter running the length of the left wall, six stools along it, and four small tables along the right, each barely big enough for two plates and two elbows. The lighting is a warm amber that comes from paper lanterns hanging at intervals and a string of exposed bulbs that cast everything in the golden-hour glow of a memory you haven't made yet. It smells like charcoal and soy sauce and grilled things.

We take a table in the back corner. The table is so small that our knees touch immediately, and this time neither of us pretends it's an accident. Her knee presses against mine and stays.

<<kira "So.">>

<<eli "So.">>

<<kira "We're doing this.">>

<<eli "We're doing this.">>

A waitress comes. Kira orders in a mix of English and Japanese — edamame, gyoza, yakitori skewers, two Sapporos. I let her order because she knows what's good and I know nothing and the entire menu is in Japanese with small English subtitles that I'd need a magnifying glass to read in this lighting.

The beers arrive first. She picks hers up. Holds it out.

<<kira "To... I don't know. What are we toasting to?">>

<<eli "Click-through rates.">>

She kicks me under the table. I feel her platform sneaker connect with my shin and the contact sends the same electric jolt it did at lunch, except this time it's not a secret — this time we're sitting in the golden light of an izakaya and she's wearing lip gloss and I called her hot forty-five minutes ago and everything that was subtext is becoming text.

<<kira "To honesty.">>

<<eli "To honesty.">>

We clink. Drink. The Sapporo is cold and clean and I feel it immediately because I've eaten one microwave burrito and a bowl of ramen today and my body has the alcohol tolerance of a house cat.

<<eli "I should eat something before this beer destroys me.">>

<<kira "The gyoza is coming. Don't die before the gyoza.">>

<<eli "No promises.">>

She laughs. The real one. Then she gets quiet. Her fingers trace the condensation on the beer glass, drawing absent patterns. I watch her do it and recognize the gesture — it's her thinking tell. She does it on stream when she's considering a play. Drawing circles on the nearest surface while her brain works.

<<kira "I need to be honest with you. More honest than the streaming stuff.">>

<<eli "Okay.">>

<<kira "I've liked you since the first time we played together online. Before we ever met in person.">>

The words land in the center of my chest like a stone dropped into still water. Ripples moving outward. Everything shifting.

<<kira "Your voice. Your humor. The way you talk to chat even when nobody's there — like you're keeping a promise to an audience that doesn't exist yet. When you're on stream, even with six viewers, you try. You always try. That's... I found that really attractive.">>

<<eli "You found my failure attractive?">>

<<kira "I found your persistence attractive. There's a difference.">>

The gyoza arrives. Six of them on a small plate, perfectly browned, steam curling from the crimped edges. Neither of us touches them.

<<kira "When we met in person I thought... I don't know what I thought. You're not my usual type.">>

The sentence hangs. She takes a breath. Her finger stops tracing the condensation. She looks at me directly — no glasses-push, no deflection, no escape hatch.

<<kira "I usually go for girls.">>

The words settle into the golden air between us. I hear them. Process them. Rearrange every assumption I've held about Kira Nomura in the space of a heartbeat.

<<eli "Girls?">>

<<kira "...yeah. I've dated girls before. A couple times. One in high school — my best friend, classic story, very dramatic, very lesbian. One in my first year of college — this girl from my dorm who played bass in a shoegaze band and had tattoo sleeves and I was completely, embarrassingly obsessed with her. That lasted four months.">>

She picks up a gyoza. Bites it. Chews. It's a stalling tactic — the same thing I do when I need a second to organize my thoughts, except hers involves well-cooked dumplings and mine involves microwave burritos.

<<kira "I've always known I liked girls. That was never complicated. I like the way women look, the way they talk, the way they smell. I like softness and curves and the specific kind of intelligence that women have when they're not performing for men.">>

She takes another bite. Swallows.

<<kira "But then I met you. Online first, and you were funny and sad and honest in a way that I couldn't stop thinking about. And then in person, and you were... you.">>

<<eli "Greasy-haired and broke?">>

<<kira "Shut up. You were real. You're the most real person I've ever met. Everyone on the internet is performing — I'm performing, my viewers are performing, the whole ecosystem is a stage. And you just... aren't. You can't perform. You're terrible at it. It's your worst quality and also the thing I like most about you.">>

She puts the gyoza down. Half-eaten, abandoned on the edge of the plate.

<<kira "You were a guy and I liked you and I just... shelved it. Filed it under 'complicated' and left it there. Told myself it was friendship. Told myself the heart emojis were just how I talk. Told myself 'good boy' was a bit.">>

<<eli "...was it a bit?">>

<<kira "Eli. I typed 'good boy' with a smirk emoji and then stared at my phone for four minutes waiting for your response. Does that sound like a bit?">>

<<eli "You waited four minutes?">>

<<kira "You sent 'lol.' Three letters. I sent you the most charged two words in my vocabulary and you sent me 'lol.' I almost threw my phone in the bath.">>

<<eli "I panicked.">>

<<kira "You panicked! I could tell! That's what made it worse — I knew you liked it and you STILL sent 'lol.' You absolute coward.">>

<<eli "In my defense, my brain shut down.">>

<<kira "Good. My brain shut down for eighteen months. Welcome to the club.">>

The yakitori arrives. We ignore it. The untouched food accumulates between us like evidence that something more important is happening here than eating.

<<eli "So you're... bisexual?">>

<<kira "I don't love labels. But yeah, if you need one, bi works. I like people. I like specific people, specifically. Gender is... part of the equation, but it's not the whole equation. With girls it's immediate — I see a beautiful woman and my brain just goes 'yes.' With guys it's slower. It's like I need to know them first. I need to hear how they think before I can feel anything.">>

<<eli "Demisexual? For guys, I mean.">>

<<kira "Maybe? I've read about it. The terminology doesn't matter to me as much as the reality, and the reality is—">>

She reaches across the table. The small table, the tiny two-top with the beer glasses and the untouched yakitori and the half-eaten gyoza. Her hand lands on mine. Small over large. Warm over cold. Her fingers curl around the edge of my palm and I feel the pixel-heart tattoo press against my skin, the raised ink a tiny Braille declaration.

<<kira "The reality is that I like you. I've liked you for a long time and I was too scared to say it because you're my friend and I didn't want to lose that. And because I wasn't sure if I was allowed to — like, as a girl who mostly likes girls, am I allowed to also like a boy? Is that valid? It sounds stupid when I say it out loud but it was a whole thing in my head for months.">>

<<eli "It's valid.">>

<<kira "I know it's valid intellectually. But at 2 AM when you're lying in bed analyzing every interaction you've had with someone and trying to figure out if the butterflies are real or if you're just lonely and touch-starved and projecting — intellectual validity doesn't help much.">>

<<eli "I do that too. The 2 AM thing.">>

<<kira "You do?">>

<<eli "Kira. I've been lying in bed at 2 AM replaying your messages and analyzing emoji placement for a year and a half. You sent me a red heart once and I spent three days trying to figure out if red means more than pink.">>

<<kira "It does.">>

<<eli "...it does?">>

<<kira "Red is the real one. Pink is friends. Red is... not friends.">>

<<eli "You sent me red.">>

<<kira "I sent you red.">>

Our hands are interlaced now. My fingers — large, rough, the nails bitten down, the skin around the thumbnails raw from anxious picking — threaded through hers. Small, smooth, warm. The size difference is absurd. Her hand disappears inside mine like it was designed to be held there. I can feel her pulse through her wrist, or maybe that's my pulse, or maybe it's the same pulse — two hearts running the same tempo in a tiny izakaya in January.

<<eli "I don't know what I'm doing.">>

<<kira "Literally no one does.">>

<<eli "No, I mean — I have twelve viewers and forty-seven dollars in my bank account and I can't afford this beer and I'm wearing the same joggers I wore yesterday. You have a career. You have a ring light. You have cable management. I don't have anything.">>

<<kira "You have me.">>

<<eli "Kira.">>

<<kira "What? It's true. You have me. If you want me.">>

Her thumb traces a circle on the back of my hand. Slow. Deliberate. The same gesture she was doing on the beer glass, except now the surface is my skin and the pattern she's drawing is something I can feel moving through every nerve in my arm and down my spine and into the part of my chest where the dangerous feelings live.

<<eli "I want you. I've wanted you since the first time you laughed on a Discord call and I had to mute myself because the sound you made did something to my heartbeat that I couldn't explain to a doctor.">>

<<kira "...that might be the most romantic thing anyone's ever said to me, and it's about a Discord call.">>

<<eli "Our entire relationship has been on Discord. Romance adapts to the medium.">>

She laughs. The real one. The crinkle-nose, glasses-slip, hand-over-mouth laugh that I've been hoarding in my memory like a miser counting coins. Except this time I'm not hearing it through earbuds on a late-night call. I'm hearing it across a table, in person, with her hand in mine, and it sounds different at close range. Fuller. Warmer. Realer.

We eat. Finally. The yakitori is good — charcoal-grilled chicken on skewers, glazed with tare, smoky and sweet. The gyoza is better. The beer is making me warm, not drunk, the pleasant looseness that comes from one drink on a mostly-empty stomach in a room with good lighting and a girl who just told you she likes you.

We talk for three hours.

Not about streaming. About everything else.

Her family — Japanese-American, second generation, her grandparents on her mom's side immigrated to California in the '80s. Her dad is white, from Michigan, an engineer who's supportive but perplexed by her career choice. Her mom runs a small bakery in the suburbs and sends Kira care packages of mochi and handwritten notes in a mix of English and Japanese. She has a younger brother, sixteen, who thinks streaming is embarrassing but secretly watches all her VODs.

<<kira "He'll never admit it but I can see his username in my viewer list. 'NotKirasBrother.' Very subtle.">>

My family — or the absence of one. Dad left before I remember. Mom works two jobs in another state and sends money every couple of months with a text that says "hope you're eating ok" which is both the most and least she's capable of giving. No siblings. No grandparents I'm close to. Marcus is the closest thing I have to family, and he fills the role with a combination of insults and cheese puffs that somehow works.

<<eli "I don't have a sad backstory. It's not dramatic. My mom's just... far. In every way.">>

<<kira "That can be its own kind of sad.">>

<<eli "I guess. I don't think about it much.">>

<<kira "You think about everything much.">>

She's right. I do. I think about everything constantly — the ceiling crack, the viewer count, the $47, the gap between my life and the lives of people who seem to know how to live. The difference is that I think about these things the way you think about weather: as conditions I exist inside, not problems I can solve. Kira thinks about things like puzzles. I think about things like atmosphere.

Music — she's into J-rock, specifically a band called *Teki no Hana* that she describes with the fervor of a religious convert. I'm into whatever Spotify autoplay decides, which she finds genuinely offensive.

<<kira "You let an ALGORITHM choose your music?">>

<<eli "It knows me better than I know myself.">>

<<kira "That's the saddest thing you've said today, and you've said some sad things.">>

<<eli "At least the algorithm doesn't judge my taste.">>

<<kira "I'M judging your taste. Actively. Right now.">>

Games — we argue about tier lists for twenty minutes with the kind of genuine passion that two people who met in a gaming Discord are contractually obligated to produce. She thinks *Rift Walkers* is overrated. I threaten to leave.

<<eli "You can't call Rift Walkers overrated. Marcus and I died on Floor 14 together. That's a bond.">>

<<kira "You died on Floor 14 because you're bad.">>

<<eli "We died on Floor 14 because the boss has SIX ARMS.">>

<<kira "Skill issue.">>

<<eli "You're cruel.">>

<<kira "You like it.">>

I do. God help me, I do. The banter is different now — the same rhythm we've always had, the same call-and-response, but everything lands differently when your hand is still warm from holding someone's. Every joke has a weight to it. Every eye-roll is a caress.

And throughout all of it — the stories, the arguments, the food, the beer — there's a slow, circling approach toward the thing we said in her apartment. We get close to it. We veer away. We come back. Like two people walking around a fire, feeling the heat, daring each other to step closer.

<<kira "Can I tell you something embarrassing?">>

<<eli "You practiced confessing to me in the shower. How much more embarrassing does it get?">>

<<kira "I once opened your stream at 3 AM on my laptop and fell asleep listening to you play. Like... on purpose. I put you on as background noise because your voice—">>

She stops. Her cheeks, already warm from the beer, go fully pink.

<<kira "Your voice is calming. When you're not performing, when you're just talking to nobody, you have this... low, quiet thing. Like you're thinking out loud. I find it incredibly—">>

She catches herself. Picks up her beer. Drinks. Puts it down.

<<eli "Incredibly what?">>

<<kira "Don't make me say it.">>

<<eli "You made me say mine.">>

<<kira "You said 'hot.' While I was talking about thumbnails. In my own apartment.">>

<<eli "And now it's your turn.">>

She looks at me. Full eye contact, no glasses-push, no escape. The amber light from the paper lanterns makes her eyes look like they contain something molten.

<<kira "Attractive. I find your voice incredibly attractive. The real one, not the stream one. The 3 AM one. The one you use when you think nobody's listening.">>

<<eli "Nobody IS listening. That's the problem.">>

<<kira "I was listening.">>

The words sit between us on the small table, next to the empty skewer plate and the beer glasses with their condensation rings. *I was listening.* Past tense and present tense and future tense all at once. She was listening when no one else was. She is listening now. She will keep listening.

<<eli "You know, when Marc told me you liked me, I didn't believe him.">>

<<kira "Marc said that?">>

<<eli "Yesterday. At the coffee shop. He said 'she likes you you' with emphasis on the second you. Like there's a special you that's different from the regular you.">>

<<kira "There is.">>

<<eli "What's the difference?">>

<<kira "Regular you is when someone likes the idea of you. Like, your persona, your content, whatever you project. Special you is when someone likes the part you don't show anyone. The 3 AM part. The penguin-knees-at-4 AM part. The part that picks at your thumbnails when you're anxious and eats microwave burritos for dinner and stares at the ceiling instead of sleeping.">>

<<eli "How do you know about the thumbnail thing?">>

<<kira "Eli. We've been on video calls. I notice things.">>

She notices things. She notices the thing I do with my hands when I'm nervous. She notices my 3 AM voice. She notices the difference between my stream self and my real self, and she chose the real one.

Something in my chest unlocks. Not breaks — I've had enough breaking. This is the opposite. A lock turning. A door opening. A room I didn't know existed in my own body suddenly lit up and warm and full of something I'm terrified to name.

<<eli "I'm scared.">>

I say it without planning to. Like the "hot" thing, but deeper. Realer.

<<kira "Of what?">>

<<eli "That this is the best day I've had in years and it's going to end and tomorrow I'll be back in my apartment with my twelve viewers and my ceiling crack and this will feel like something I imagined.">>

She puts her beer down. Slides her hand across the table, palm up, fingers open. An invitation.

<<kira "Then don't imagine it. Be here.">>

I take her hand. Our fingers interlace again. The geometry is the same as before — large and small, rough and smooth — but this time I hold tighter. This time she holds back.

We leave at 10:30. The izakaya is closing and we're the last ones there, the staff pointedly wiping down tables around us while we pretend not to notice. Kira pays. I try to argue. She gives me the look — the jaw-set, eyes-forward, topic-is-closed look — and I let her.

Outside, the January night is bitter. The cold has teeth tonight, the kind that get through the hoodie and the grey shirt and the skin and settle into the bone. My breath makes clouds. So does hers. We stand on the sidewalk in front of *Kitsune* and the street is empty and the city is quiet in the way cities get at this hour — not silent, but muted, the background hum dialed down to something like a whisper.

<<kira "I'll drive you home.">>

<<eli "You don't have to—">>

<<kira "I swear to God if you say I don't have to one more time—">>

<<eli "Okay. Drive me home.">>

We walk to her car. Two blocks. The Civic is where she left it, outside her building, and the walk is short but the cold makes it feel longer. Our hands are in our respective pockets — it's too cold for hand-holding, too cold for exposed skin — but we walk close. Close enough that our shoulders brush with every other step. Close enough that I can feel her warmth even through two layers of January air.

She unlocks the car. We get in. The cherry blossom air freshener hits me and this time it doesn't just smell like her car — it smells like a memory being formed, the scent that will always mean *this night, this person, this moment*.

She starts the engine. The heater coughs to life. We sit in the warming car while the windshield defrosts, the glass clearing in slow circles from the vents outward.

<<kira "I had a really good time.">>

<<eli "Me too.">>

<<kira "Like... a really good time. An embarrassingly good time. Like, I'm going to go home and journal about this.">>

<<eli "You journal?">>

<<kira "Don't judge me.">>

<<eli "I'm not. I'm going to go home and lie on a floor mattress and stare at a ceiling crack about this. Everyone processes differently.">>

She laughs. Soft this time. Not the burst — the other one, the quiet one, the one that sounds like a sigh that decided to be happy instead. She's looking at the windshield, watching the frost clear. Her profile is lit by the dashboard glow — green and blue light tracing the edge of her nose, her chin, the curve of her lower lip with its shiny chapstick that we both know is lip gloss.

<<kira "Eli?">>

<<eli "Yeah?">>

She turns to face me. In the small car, in the dark, with the heater breathing warm air between us and the cherry blossom hanging in the space, she turns her whole body toward me and she's close — closer than the izakaya, closer than the ramen shop, closer than she's ever been. I can see the individual eyelashes behind her glasses. I can see the faint freckle near her left eyebrow that I've only ever noticed in high-res selfies. I can see her pulse in her neck.

<<kira "I'm going to do something and I need you to not panic.">>

<<eli "That's exactly the kind of sentence that makes people panic.">>

<<kira "Eli.">>

<<eli "I'm not panicking.">>

She leans across the center console. The movement is slow and deliberate — Kira doesn't do anything without intention — but there's a tremor in it, a vibration in her hands as they find the front of my hoodie and curl into the fabric. She's shaking. The girl with 1,100 viewers and $487 sessions and a career and a ring light and cable couture is shaking.

Her face is close. Two inches. I can feel her breath — warm, slightly sweet from the beer, landing on my lips. Her eyes are huge behind the glasses, the brown irises catching dashboard light, and she's looking at me the way she looks at her stream in the seconds before she goes live — focused, terrified, committed.

<<kira "Hi.">>
