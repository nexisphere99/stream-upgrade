# DAY 2 — "The Gap" (Part 3: Standard Route — The Birth of ElliePlayz)

---

I laugh.

It comes out wrong — too fast, too high, a bark of sound that doesn't match my face or my voice or anything about me. It's the emergency laugh. The one I keep behind glass labeled BREAK IN CASE OF EMOTIONAL VULNERABILITY. My hand comes up and runs through my hair — the hotel-shampoo hair, the hair I washed for her — and I force a grin that feels like a mask being stapled to my face.

<<eli "Kidding, kidding. Anyway, the algorithm — you were saying something about click-through rates?">>

The moment shatters. I can hear it — a crystalline sound, like a glass dropped on tile, except it's happening inside my chest. The charged air between us deflates. The three seconds of silence collapse into nothing, vacuum-sealed and buried.

Kira blinks. The pink on her cheeks doesn't vanish instantly — it lingers, a ghost of the thing that almost happened, fading slowly like a sunset nobody photographed. She pushes her glasses up. Once. Twice. The nervous tell, firing in rapid succession.

<<kira "...right. Click-through rates. Yeah.">>

She clears her throat. Recrosses her legs. Turns back to the monitor and the dashboard with its $487 and her voice picks up the thread of algorithmic bias like nothing happened, like the air between us didn't just vibrate at a frequency that could shatter glass, like I didn't just call her hot and then take it back with the speed and grace of a man defusing a bomb he built himself.

Her voice is steady. Professional. The streamer voice. The wall.

I sit on the couch and nod and say "yeah" and "that makes sense" and "interesting" in all the right places, and behind every word my brain is running a parallel track that sounds like this: *you coward. You absolute coward. You had the moment. The moment was RIGHT THERE. She was looking at you and her cheeks were pink and she pushed her glasses up twice and you could have held her gaze and said "I meant it" and instead you laughed. You LAUGHED. You wrapped the most honest thing you've said in months inside a joke and handed it back like defective merchandise.*

The algorithm talk continues. I absorb maybe 40% of it. The rest of my cognitive capacity is devoted to a postmortem of the last thirty seconds — replaying the look on her face when I said "hot," the specific pink of her cheeks, the way her lips parted, the 0.5-second window where she was waiting for me to mean it and I chose, consciously, to not mean it.

Because what would happen if I meant it? If I'd held her gaze? If I'd followed "it's hot" with "you're hot" and let the words stand without the emergency laugh and the hair-run and the "kidding"? What then? A girl with 1,100 viewers and a ring light and cable management looks at a guy with $47 and a ceiling crack and says... what? "Thanks, you too"? "That's sweet but we're friends"? The math doesn't work. The math has never worked. And the gap between her dashboard and mine is a canyon I can see the bottom of, and the bottom is my face.

<<eli "Sure.">>

The word comes out flat. She notices — I see her eyes flick to me, quick, the micro-expression of someone clocking a tone shift — but she doesn't press it. She sets up the collab the same way she would have regardless of which choice I made: Discord call, my phone camera as a secondary overlay, her stream layout accommodating my grainy rectangle in the upper right corner like a footnote.

The dashboard with its $487 glowing on the monitor behind her, the ring light still warm from being on, and my face in the dead webcam preview like a before photo in an ad for a life I can't afford.

I did the thing I always do — closed the door, boarded the windows, retreated into the safe house of "I was kidding" where nothing means anything and nothing can reach me.

<<kira "The streaming thing. The numbers. The gap. I know it feels like it's about you — like your face, your voice, your personality. But it's not. It's the system. And systems can be hacked.">>

<<eli "What does that mean?">>

<<kira "I don't know yet. But think about it.">>

I nod. Open the door. The January cold rushes in, obliterating the cherry blossom and the warmth and everything the car contained. I step out. Close the door. The Civic pulls away. Red taillights in the grey street. Gone.

I stand on the sidewalk for ten seconds, breathing cold air, my hands in my hoodie pockets, the left hand finding the quarter-sized hole and pushing through it. The building looms. The window of my apartment is dark.

I go inside.

$47 in one hour.

$3.50 in two hours as Eli.

$47 ÷ 1 hour = $47/hour as Ellie.
$3.50 ÷ 2 hours = $1.75/hour as Eli.

That's a 2,685% increase. Same person. Same skill. Same game. Same platform. Different voice. Different name. Different gender, or at least the performed suggestion of one.

*If I were a guy with the exact same personality, the exact same humor, the exact same stream quality — I'd be you, Eli.*

Kira said that. Today. In her apartment. In front of her dashboard. She said it as a diagnosis. A statement of fact. The system rewards a specific thing and I am not that thing.

But Ellie is.

Ellie — a voice, a name, a pink-and-purple gradient background and a cursor font — IS that thing. She doesn't have a face. She doesn't have a body. She doesn't exist. But she has 47 followers and $47 and 85 peak viewers and a chat full of strangers who called her cute and queen and cracked and told her they'd be back tomorrow.

Tomorrow. They said they'd be back tomorrow.

I lie in the dark and I stare at the ceiling crack and I think about tomorrow. About the two streams — the one where I'm Eli, talking into a void, performing for DarkSlayer and bots, earning nothing. <<eli "you were right about everything">>

<<eli "the system. the gap. all of it. you were right.">>

<<kira "eli... are you okay?">>

<<eli "yeah. im okay. just thinking.">>

<<kira "about what?">>

<<eli "about systems. and how to hack them.">>

<<kira "...that's either very inspiring or very concerning and i can't tell which">>

<<eli "maybe both">>

<<kira "just don't do anything weird okay? i worry about you">>

<<kira "promise?">>

<<eli "promise">>

<<kira "okay. goodnight eli. talk tomorrow? ❤️">>

Red heart. The real one. She's still sending them. Even after I laughed. Even after I took it back. She's still giving me the red heart, the one that means not-friends, the one that means something I'm too much of a coward to receive.

<<eli "night kira">>

No heart. I don't send one. The message goes out bare, the same way it always does, two words without punctuation, the emotional equivalent of a closed door.

I put the phone down.

The fridge hums.

The ceiling crack grows.

I close my eyes and I don't dream about Kira. I don't dream about the kiss that didn't happen or the hand I didn't hold or the moment I killed with a laugh.

I dream about a number.

Forty-seven.
