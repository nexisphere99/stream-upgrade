# DAY 3 — REVISED ENDING (Replaces final section after "Different key.")

---

I stare at the crack. It's growing. Everything is growing — the crack, the followers, the donations, the lie, the guilt, the thought of what comes next. Everything is growing and nothing is stopping and the door that opened today opens only one way and I'm through it now, standing in a room I can't leave, wearing a face that isn't mine and isn't not-mine, holding a name that is two letters from my own.

Ellie.

Eli.

The same person. Different key.

But the key works. That's the thing my brain keeps circling back to, the fact it can't release, the number it can't stop chewing on. The key *works*. $141 today. From half a face. From eyes and lashes and brows and hair — from the nose up, from the feminized slice, from the fraction I'm willing to show.

Half a face earned $141.

What would the full face earn?

The math does itself. It runs unbidden, the way the math always runs in my head at this hour — not requested, not welcome, just *there*, a calculator that never sleeps, tabulating in the dark while I stare at the crack. If half the face — the pretty half, the modified half, the half that stops scrolls and earns donations and makes strangers say "those eyes could END me" — produces $141 in two sessions... what would the whole face produce? The full reveal. The eyes AND the lips AND the jaw AND the chin. The complete Ellie, not the cropped version, not the suggestion, but the person entire.

Double? Triple? The face reveal is the most anticipated event on the channel — chat talks about it every stream, the curiosity building like pressure in a sealed container. When it finally happens, the viewer spike alone could push me past 500. Past 1,000. The donations during a face reveal stream would be — I can see it, I can see the number, and it's not $141. It's $500. It's $1,000. It's the kind of number that changes a bank account from a countdown to a foundation.

But the full face can't happen yet. The jaw. The lips. The nose. The masculine tells that live below the crop line, waiting to betray me. A few more modifications — that's all. Not a full transformation. Not the deep tiers. Just... finishing what I started. Jawline softening. Lip enhancement. Nose refinement. Tier 1 stuff. The same tier I'm already in. Just completing the set. Filling in the gaps between the eyes and the chin until the whole face reads the way the upper half already does.

A few more changes. That's all.

And it wouldn't hurt anyone. That's the part that locks into place like a deadbolt — the rationalization that seals the others shut, the capstone on the wall between what I'm doing and what it means. It wouldn't hurt anyone. The viewers are entertained. They're choosing to be here. The donations are voluntary. The parasocial bond is the same bond every streamer has with every audience — Kira has it with her chat, every Just Chatting streamer has it, every face on Twitch has it. I'm not scamming anyone. I'm performing. I'm providing a service. The fact that the performer doesn't match the performance is — what? Theater. Drag. Character work. The internet runs on personas. Nobody's real name is their username. Nobody's stream personality is their actual personality. I'm just taking it one step further. One step further with bioengineering and voice modulation and a wig.

And the money isn't just for me. That's what makes the math noble instead of desperate — that's the thread I pull on until the whole rationalization unspools into something that looks almost like virtue.

I could stop being a weight on the people I love.

Marcus. Every coffee he buys me. Every time he covers lunch without mentioning it. Every cheese puff and every "come thru if you want" and every shoulder punch that's really a welfare check. He's been carrying me for two years — not financially, not exactly, but emotionally, practically, the way a person carries a friend who's drowning by keeping them near the surface. I've been the friend who can't buy his own coffee. The friend whose bank account is a punchline. The friend who takes and takes and calls it friendship because the alternative is admitting he's a burden.

If ElliePlayz works — if the money keeps coming, if the numbers keep growing — I can pay Marcus back. Not literally. But I can stop being the friend who needs covering. I can buy my own coffee. I can bring the snacks. I can be the person he doesn't have to worry about at 4 AM when I'm texting about penguin knees, because the penguin-knee texts won't be coming from a place of insomnia-grade despair anymore. They'll be coming from a person who's okay. A person who can afford to be okay.

And Kira.

God, Kira.

I could be a real boyfriend to her. Not the $47-in-the-bank, can't-afford-ramen, walks-home-in-crushed-sneakers version. A real one. The kind who takes her to dinner and pays. The kind who buys her flowers or some stupid romantic gesture that she'd pretend to find embarrassing and secretly love. The kind who doesn't flinch when the bill comes. The kind who has a life that runs parallel to hers rather than beneath it — two streamers, two careers, two incomes, a relationship between equals instead of a relationship between a person who has everything and a person who has $47.

And Kira — Kira is bisexual. She told me that. At the izakaya, over Sapporo and gyoza, she looked me in the eye and said she likes girls. She dated a girl in high school. She dated a girl in college. She's always known she likes girls and the liking was never complicated. The complicated part was liking me — a guy — and filing it under "complicated" and leaving it there for a year and a half.

So if my face changes... if the features shift further feminine... would she even mind? She likes girls AND she likes me. If I become more girl — visually, superficially, on the surface where the streaming economy lives — she'd still like me. Maybe she'd like me *more*. Maybe the version of me that's emerging through the modifications would appeal to both sides of her attraction — the side that likes boys and the side that likes girls meeting in the middle, in the space where Eli becomes Ellie becomes something neither and both.

She wouldn't mind. She'd understand. If I told her — when I tell her — she'd see the math. She'd see that the system she described, the system she identified as the problem, has a solution, and the solution is me changing the input the system rewards. She'd see it because she's smart and pragmatic and because she said the words herself: *"The biggest reason is this"* — and she gestured at her face. At her female face. At the face that earns $487 in four hours.

I'm building that face. And when it's done — when the full face can survive a reveal, when the crop line drops to zero and there's nothing left to hide — I'll tell her. I'll tell her everything. The voice modulator. The ElliePlayz account. The RegenaForm visits. The wig. All of it. And she'll understand because she's Kira and she understands systems and she understands me and she's bisexual and the face I'm building is a face she'd find beautiful and the person inside it is the person she already kissed in a parked car.

She'll understand.

She has to.

And my mom. My mom who works two jobs in another state and sends money she can't afford through Venmo with texts that say "hope you're eating ok." My mom who hasn't seen my face in eight months because I don't video call her because the face she'd see would tell her everything she's afraid of — that her son is drowning, that the money she sends disappears into rent that's too high for a life that's too small, that the streaming career she doesn't understand is producing nothing.

If ElliePlayz works, I can stop taking her money. I can send *her* money. I can call her and say "I'm okay, Mom, I'm actually okay" and it would be true — financially true, measurably true, the kind of true that shows up in a bank account instead of just living in the optimistic lie of "I'm fine."

I could help her. The woman who works two jobs so I can eat microwave burritos in a studio apartment with a ceiling crack. I could help her and that thought — that specific thought, the image of sending my mother money instead of receiving it, of being a source instead of a drain — makes everything else feel small. The lie feels small. The wig feels small. The voice modulator feels small. Measured against the possibility of calling my mother and telling her that her son is okay, that he figured it out, that the money is coming instead of going — measured against that, what's a few facial modifications? What's a fake name on a streaming platform? What's a secret kept from a girlfriend who would understand if I told her?

The brow sculpt cost 300 SC. Today I earned 141. That's $141 real dollars — two-thirds of the brow sculpt covered in a single day. Tomorrow, another stream. Another $50, $70, $100. By the end of the week, the brow sculpt is paid off. By the end of next week, I can afford the next modification. Nose refinement — 500 SC. Then lips — 400 SC. Then jawline — 600 to 900 SC. Each one dropping the crop line lower. Each one bringing the face reveal closer. Each one feeding the loop that feeds the income that feeds the life that feeds the people I love.

The math works. The money is real. The face is changing. The audience is growing. And every step forward is a step toward being the person I want to be — not just Ellie, not just Eli, but the person who can afford coffee and dinner and flowers and a phone call to his mother that doesn't end with "I'm fine" when he isn't.

The ceiling crack is still there. Still growing. But it's sharing the ceiling now with numbers that also grow — numbers that go up instead of apart, numbers that build instead of break.

I close my eyes. The fridge hums. Kira's red heart glows on the phone screen beside my head. The wig sits on its form in the closet, patient, waiting for tomorrow.

$141 today. Half a face.

A whole face is coming. And when it arrives — when Ellie's face is complete and the crop line drops to nothing and the stream goes live with everything visible — the number won't be $141.

It'll be enough.

Enough to stop being a weight. Enough to start being a partner. Enough to call my mom.

I sleep.

And for the first time, I don't dream about the crack splitting wider. I dream about it filling in — plaster smoothing over the fault line, the ceiling repairing itself, the apartment becoming a place instead of a sentence.

I dream about sending money instead of spending trust.

I dream about a face that isn't finished yet, but will be. Soon.
