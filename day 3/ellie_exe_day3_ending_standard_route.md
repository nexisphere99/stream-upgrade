# DAY 3 — REVISED ENDING (STANDARD ROUTE)
## Replaces final section after "Different key."

---

I stare at the crack. It's growing. Everything is growing — the crack, the followers, the donations, the lie, the guilt, the thought of what comes next. Everything is growing and nothing is stopping and the door that opened today opens only one way and I'm through it now, standing in a room I can't leave, wearing a face that isn't mine and isn't not-mine, holding a name that is two letters from my own.

Ellie.

Eli.

The same person. Different key.

But the key works. That's the thing my brain keeps circling back to, the fact it can't release, the number it can't stop chewing on. The key *works*. $210 today. From half a face. From eyes and lashes and brows and hair — from the nose up, from the feminized slice, from the fraction I'm willing to show.

Half a face earned $210.

What would the full face earn?

The math does itself. It runs unbidden, the way the math always runs in my head at this hour — not requested, not welcome, just *there*, a calculator that never sleeps, tabulating in the dark while I stare at the crack. If half the face — the pretty half, the modified half, the half that stops scrolls and earns donations and makes strangers say "those eyes could END me" — produces $210 in two sessions... what would the whole face produce? The full reveal. The eyes AND the lips AND the jaw AND the chin. The complete Ellie, not the cropped version, not the suggestion, but the person entire.

Double? Triple? The face reveal is the most anticipated event on the channel — chat talks about it every stream, the curiosity building like pressure in a sealed container. When it finally happens, the viewer spike alone could push me past 500. Past 1,000. The donations during a face reveal stream would be — I can see it, I can see the number, and it's not $210. It's $500. It's $1,000. It's the kind of number that changes a bank account from a countdown to a foundation.

But the full face can't happen yet. The jaw. The lips. The nose. The masculine tells that live below the crop line, waiting to betray me. A few more modifications — that's all. Not a full transformation. Not the deep tiers. Just... finishing what I started. Jawline softening. Lip enhancement. Nose refinement. Tier 1 stuff. The same tier I'm already in. Just completing the set. Filling in the gaps between the eyes and the chin until the whole face reads the way the upper half already does.

A few more changes. That's all. A few more changes to show the full face.

And it wouldn't hurt anyone.

That's the part that locks into place like a deadbolt — the rationalization that seals the others shut, the capstone on the wall between what I'm doing and what it means. It wouldn't hurt anyone. The viewers are entertained. They're choosing to be here. The donations are voluntary. The parasocial bond is the same bond every streamer has with every audience — Kira has it with her chat, every Just Chatting streamer has it, every face on Twitch has it. I'm not scamming anyone. I'm performing. I'm providing a service. The fact that the performer doesn't match the performance is — what? Theater. Drag. Character work. The internet runs on personas. Nobody's real name is their username. Nobody's stream personality is their actual personality. I'm just taking it one step further. One step further with bioengineering and voice modulation and a wig.

A few more changes to the face won't hurt a single soul on this earth. The viewers get their content. The algorithm gets its engagement. RegenaForm gets its StreamCoin. And I get a life.

And the money — the money isn't just for me. That's what makes the math noble instead of desperate. That's the thread I pull on until the whole rationalization unspools into something that looks almost like virtue.

I could stop being a weight.

Marcus. Every coffee he buys me without mentioning it. Every time he covers lunch and changes the subject before I can offer to split. Every cheese puff and every "come thru if u want" and every shoulder punch that's really a welfare check disguised as friendship. He's been carrying me for two years — not financially, not exactly, but emotionally, practically, the way a person carries a friend who's drowning by keeping them near the surface without ever acknowledging the water. I've been the friend who can't buy his own coffee. The friend whose bank account is a punchline. The friend who takes and takes and calls it friendship because the alternative is admitting he's a burden.

$210 in one day. If this continues — if the numbers hold, if the growth sustains — I can pay Marcus back. Not literally. He'd never accept money and he'd be insulted if I offered. But I can stop being the friend who needs covering. I can buy my own coffee. I can bring the snacks to his apartment instead of eating his. I can show up with a Tupperware of something that isn't a microwave burrito and say "I made this" and he'd look at me sideways and say "since when do you cook" and I'd say "since I could afford groceries" and neither of us would need to acknowledge what changed because the change would be visible in the Tupperware and the groceries and the fact that I'm standing in his kitchen with food I bought with money I earned.

And Kira. Not as a girlfriend — that moment passed, I laughed it off, the "it's hot" died in the emergency chuckle — but as a friend. The friend who buys me ramen and drives me places and sends red hearts I don't return. The friend who told me the truth about the system and then watched me fail to use the information. Except I am using it now. She just doesn't know.

If ElliePlayz works, I could tell her. Maybe. Eventually. I can picture the conversation — sitting in her apartment, Packet on the armrest judging us both, and me saying: *"Remember when you told me the system rewards a specific input? I found a way to change the input."* And she'd look at me with those analytical eyes, the ones that see everything, and she'd understand. Because Kira understands systems. She identified the problem. She'd respect the solution, even if the solution is unconventional, even if it involves a wig and a voice modulator and a clinic four blocks from my apartment.

She'd understand. She might even be impressed. The pragmatist in her — the part that writes essays about parasocial economics for fun — would see the elegance of it. The same system that punished Eli rewards Ellie. Same person. Different packaging. Market arbitrage.

Marc would understand too. Eventually. Once there's something to show — once the numbers are undeniable, once the income is real and sustained, once I'm not presenting a theory but a result. Marc doesn't care about methods. He cares about outcomes. He cares about whether I'm okay. And if the outcome of a wig and some facial modifications and a fake name is that I'm okay — actually, financially, measurably okay — he'd nod and say "wild" and ask if I'd eaten today and the conversation would move on because that's what Marcus does. He absorbs. He adjusts. He carries on.

I'll tell them both. When the time is right. When the face is further along. When the numbers are big enough that the confession comes with evidence attached.

And my mom.

God. My mom.

My mom who works two jobs in another state — one at a grocery store, one doing night cleaning at an office building — and sends money she can't afford through Venmo every other month with texts that say "hope you're eating ok." Four words. The maximum emotional bandwidth of a woman who loves her son but doesn't know how to say it and doesn't have the energy to learn because both of her energies — the first shift and the second shift — are spent by the time she gets home to an apartment that's probably not much bigger than mine.

She sends me $200 every two months. $200 she earns standing on her feet for eight hours scanning groceries and then standing on her feet for four more hours pushing a vacuum through cubicle farms. $200 that crosses the distance between her state and mine and lands in my Venmo like a care package from a war zone, except the war zone is just America and the casualties are just dignity.

$210. I earned $210 today. In one day. Sitting in a chair. Talking into a camera. Playing a video game.

My mother earns that in two months of supplemental labor on top of two jobs.

If ElliePlayz works — if the money continues, if the growth holds — I can stop taking her money. I can send *her* money. I can open Venmo and reverse the arrow and type "hope YOU'RE eating ok" and attach a number that's big enough to matter and she'd see it on her phone between shifts and she might cry or she might just nod the way she nods at things she doesn't fully understand but recognizes as good.

I could call her. Pick up the phone and video call and when she sees my face — whatever face it is by then, whatever configuration the modifications have produced — she'd see a person who's okay. Not the gaunt, pale, acne-marked, dark-circled face she's afraid to see. A face that has color. That has rest. That has the specific luminosity of a person who isn't drowning anymore.

She doesn't need to know how the money was earned. She doesn't need to know about Ellie or RegenaForm or the voice modulator. She just needs to know her son is okay. And if the path to okay runs through a wig and a webcam and a name that's two letters different from his own — that's a path. That's more path than he's had in eight months. That's more path than the ceiling crack and the microwave burritos and the $12.50 monthly income offered.

The brow sculpt cost $300. Today I earned $210. Two-thirds of the cost covered in a single day. Tomorrow, another stream. Another $100, $150, $200. By the end of the week, the brow sculpt is paid off. By the end of next week, I can afford the next modification.

Nose refinement — $500. Then lips — $400. Then jawline — $600 to $900. Each one dropping the crop line lower. Each one bringing the face reveal closer. Each one feeding the loop that feeds the income that feeds the life that feeds the people who've been feeding me.

The math works. The money is real. The face is changing. The audience is growing.

A few more changes. A few more trips to Suite 12. A few more sessions in the chair with the cool gel and the warmth and the cells restructuring beneath the skin.

It won't hurt anyone. The viewers get entertainment. RegenaForm gets paid. Marc gets a friend who can buy coffee. Kira gets a friend who figured it out. Mom gets a son who's okay.

And I get a life.

The ceiling crack is still there. Still growing. But it's sharing the ceiling now with numbers that also grow — numbers that go up instead of apart, numbers that build instead of break.

I close my eyes. The fridge hums. The wig sits on its form in the closet, patient, waiting for tomorrow. The RegenaForm app glows on my phone beside the mattress, a small teal icon, the double helix in miniature, a door in my pocket that opens with a tap.

$210 today. Half a face.

A whole face is coming. And when it arrives — when Ellie's face is complete and the crop line drops to nothing and the stream goes live with everything visible — the number won't be $210.

It'll be enough.

Enough to stop being a weight. Enough to buy coffee. Enough to bring snacks. Enough to call my mom.

I sleep.

And for the first time, I don't dream about the crack splitting wider. I dream about it filling in — plaster smoothing over the fault line, the ceiling repairing itself, the apartment becoming a place instead of a sentence.

I dream about sending money instead of spending trust.

I dream about a face that isn't finished yet, but will be. Soon.
