# DAY 3 — "The Door" (Part 1: Morning / The Rabbit Hole) — STANDARD ROUTE

---

I wake up and the first thing I do is check the dashboard.

Not my phone. Not the time. Not the ceiling crack, which is still there, still growing, still splitting the plaster in its patient, geological way. The dashboard. I roll off the mattress, cross the two steps to the desk in my boxers and the same grey shirt I slept in, jiggle the mouse, and watch the second monitor wake with the ElliePlayz stats from last night still displayed like a stock ticker from a dream I can't believe I had.

**Peak Viewers:** 89
**Average Viewers:** 61
**New Followers:** 47
**Donations:** $47.00
**Chat Messages:** 312

It's real. I stare at the numbers the way I used to stare at the ceiling crack — with a fixed, trance-like attention that borders on devotion — except these numbers don't fill me with dread. They fill me with something else. Something that vibrates in my fingertips and my jawline and the pit of my stomach. Something I haven't felt since I first started streaming eight months ago, back when I still believed the next stream would be the one that changed everything.

Hope. Except this time the hope has evidence.

$47 in one hour. As a voice. As a name. As a pink-and-purple gradient with a cursive font. 47 followers in one session — that's 87% of what EliPlays accumulated over eight entire months. In sixty minutes, Ellie eclipsed Eli.

I sit in the desk chair. It sinks its customary inch and a half. The hydraulic wheezes. The apartment is the same — cold, dim, the fridge humming, the burrito wrappers breeding on the counter — but the numbers on the screen are a window into a different apartment, a different life, a different version of this same morning where the person sitting in this chair isn't calculating how many days until the $47 in his bank account runs out but calculating how quickly the $47 on his dashboard can multiply.

My phone buzzes. I grab it off the floor.

<<kira "hey. hope you got home safe last night.">>

Kira. The message from last night — the one I answered with "night kira" and no heart, the bare two words, the closed door. She sent this at 8:15 AM. It's now 9:47. She's been awake for an hour and a half and the first thing she did was check on me.

The warmth is there. The Kira warmth. Even in the standard route, even in the version where I laughed off calling her hot and killed the moment with cowardice, the warmth is there — muted, banked, a fire I covered with ashes but didn't extinguish. She still cares. She still sends red hearts. She still asks if I got home safe.

<<eli "yeah got home fine. late night">>

<<kira "streaming?">>

<<eli "yeah. working on some stuff">>

<<kira "the mysterious 'stuff.' one day you'll tell me what that means">>

<<eli "one day">>

<<kira "🙄 anyway. stream at noon if you want to watch. doing a Just Chatting Q&A today">>

<<eli "might tune in">>

<<kira "you better. i'm answering embarrassing questions from chat and i need moral support">>

<<eli "i'll be there">>

<<kira "❤️">>

Red heart. She's still sending them. Even after the laugh. Even after I closed the door. Kira Nomura sends red hearts the way the sun produces light — not because it's asked to, not because it's reciprocated, but because that's what it does. It's her nature. The warmth radiates outward regardless of whether it's absorbed.

I don't send one back. I send a thumbs up. The forty-year-old dad emoji. The emotional equivalent of a firm handshake when someone opens their arms for a hug.

I put the phone down. Look at the dashboard. Look at the phone. Dashboard. Phone. Two realities, two screens, two versions of who I am and what I want.

The dashboard wins.

I open the browser. Tab one: the ElliePlayz channel page. 47 followers. The number is small but it's real and it's growing and the growth happened in an hour and the trajectory, if it holds, projects into numbers that make my stomach flip. Tab two: YouTube. The VoxMorph tutorials I bookmarked last night. Tab three, which I open now with the deliberate, focused energy of a person who has identified a problem and is solving it:

Google search: "how to look feminine fast"

---

The rabbit hole opens.

It starts with makeup tutorials. Dozens of them. YouTube is overflowing with videos titled "FEMININE MAKEUP FOR BEGINNERS" and "MTF MAKEUP TRANSFORMATION" and "How to Feminize Your Face in 10 Minutes." I watch three of them in succession — women and trans women and drag queens demonstrating contouring, highlighting, the strategic application of color to reshape the way light hits bone. The techniques are impressive but impractical for my purposes: makeup is visible on camera, especially on a webcam with bad resolution, and the time investment is significant. Twenty minutes of contouring before every stream, plus the risk of smudging, sweating, the makeup shifting mid-session.

I want something more permanent. Something that doesn't wash off.

The search mutates. "How to look feminine without makeup." "Facial feminization non-surgical." "Subtle feminization techniques for streaming." The results shift from YouTube to forums — Reddit threads, niche communities, spaces where people discuss passing and presentation and the specific art of looking like someone other than who you were born as.

I read for forty-five minutes. The information is dense, detailed, and organized by the kind of people who have spent years thinking about exactly this problem. Brow shaping. Eyelash extensions. Skincare routines that change the texture and luminosity of skin. Hair — real hair, wig options, styling techniques. Each topic is a door, and behind each door is a room full of more doors, and the architecture of the rabbit hole is designed to keep you descending.

And then — on the second page of a Google search for "facial feminization fast results" — the ad.

It doesn't look like an ad. It looks like a search result, clean and authoritative, with a thumbnail image of a chrome double helix and text that reads:

**RegenaForm — Unlock Your True Form**
*DNA-based bioengineering. No scars. Full sensory integration. Consultations available.*

I click it.

The website loads and it's — beautiful. In the way that a luxury car showroom is beautiful, or a five-star hotel lobby. The design communicates money and competence and the specific brand of confidence that comes from knowing your product works. White space. Clean typography. The chrome double-helix logo rotating slowly in the header. A tagline beneath it: *"We don't change you. We unlock you."*

I scroll. Before-and-after gallery. Each transformation shown in high resolution, the images so detailed they look medical and so beautiful they look editorial. Faces shifting from one configuration to another — masculine to feminine, angular to soft, forgettable to magnetic. The changes range from subtle (a jawline refined, skin cleared) to dramatic (full facial feminization, the bone structure literally reshaped, a different person emerging from beneath the same skin).

Services. Listed in tiers. Tier 1: Facial Feminization. Eyelashes, brow sculpting, nose refinement, jawline contouring, lip enhancement, skin treatments. Tier 2: Voice Modulation (organic — not software). Tier 3: Hair. Tier 4: Body contour. Tiers 5 through 8: escalating, invasive, transformative. The prices are in Dollars, a cryptocurrency I've seen in niche forums. The conversion rate to USD is roughly 1:1.

Tier 1 starts at 500 $. Individual features are cheaper. Eyelash enhancement: 200-350 $. Brow sculpting: 300-500 $. The numbers are large for someone with $47 in the bank but small for someone who just earned $47 in an hour.

I read the FAQ.

*"Is the process reversible?"* — "All Tier 1 modifications can be revised or reversed. See our Revision Policy."

*"Does it hurt?"* — "Our DNA-based technology integrates with your own genetic material. Most clients describe the sensation as 'warm' or 'tingling.' No anesthesia required for Tier 1."

*"How long does it take?"* — "Tier 1 procedures range from 20 minutes to 2 hours. Results are immediate."

Immediate. No surgery. No scarring. No downtime. I read the word "immediate" three times and each time it sounds more like a promise and less like a claim.

At the bottom of the FAQ, a button: **Book Free Consultation**.

I click it before the rational part of my brain can file an objection. The form loads. Name: Ellie. Email: ellieplayz.stream@gmail.com (created on the spot, fingers moving faster than thought). Phone: my real number. Date: today. Time: the dropdown shows 2:30 PM available.

I submit. Confirmation page: *"Thank you, Ellie! Your consultation with Dr. Voss is confirmed for 2:30 PM today. 1440 Meridian Ave, Suite 12."*

1440 Meridian. Four blocks from my apartment. The clinic that promises to unlock my "true form" is a fifteen-minute walk from the building where I've been slowly decomposing for eight months. The proximity feels like fate or marketing. Probably marketing.

I close the browser. Open it. Close it. Open it one more time, look at the confirmation, and close it for real.

It's 11:30 AM. Three hours until the consultation.

I should stream. I should open EliPlays and grind ranked and talk to DarkSlayer and earn my $1.75 per hour and maintain the fiction that the first account still matters. But the ElliePlayz dashboard is glowing on the second monitor and the $47 is still there and the idea of going live as Eli right now feels like putting on a costume — the wrong costume, the one that doesn't fit, the one the audience doesn't want to see.

I eat a microwave burrito. Rotate at one minute. Chicken and cheese. Warm salt and texture. Monster Energy, the last one. The green can, original flavor. Breakfast of a person whose diet will kill him before the streaming does.

I watch Kira's stream for an hour. She's doing the Q&A she mentioned — sitting in front of the ring light, the bob perfect, the glasses catching the LED glow, answering questions from chat with the easy charisma of someone who's done this a thousand times. Her viewer count hovers around 950. The donations scroll. I watch her talk and laugh and perform and I feel the familiar two-part feeling: the warmth (she's incredible, she's magnetic, her laugh does things to my heartbeat) and the math (950 viewers, $12 in donations in the ten minutes I've been watching, $72 an hour, $288 in four hours, and I have $47 in the bank).

She says something that makes me sit up.

A viewer asks: *"What advice would you give to a new streamer trying to grow?"*

<<kira "Honestly? Be authentic. Find the thing that makes you *you* and lean into it. The algorithm rewards consistency and personality. Don't try to be someone you're not — the audience can tell.">>

Don't try to be someone you're not.

I close her stream. I can't watch right now. The cognitive dissonance is giving me a headache — the girl I almost kissed yesterday telling me to be authentic while I'm sitting at a desk with a voice modulator installed and a consultation booked at a bioengineering clinic to make my face look like someone else's.

12:30 PM. Two hours until RegenaForm.

I shower. Use the Marriott shampoo. Not for Kira this time — for the consultation. For Dr. Voss, whoever they are. I want to look... presentable. Not feminine, not yet, just like a person who cares about their appearance, who takes this seriously, who isn't walking in off the street in a state of visible desperation.

I put on the grey shirt. Black joggers. The hoodie. The crushed sneakers. I look in the phone camera. The face is the same — pale, dark circles, thin lips, greasy hair that the shampoo improved but didn't transform. The face that earned $3.50. The face I'm about to ask a stranger to change.

I leave the apartment at 2:10. The walk takes twelve minutes.
