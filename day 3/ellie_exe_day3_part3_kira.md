# DAY 3 — "The Door" (Part 3: Evening — Kira / The Secret Stream) — KIRA ROUTE

---

I spend the two hours before Kira arrives in a state of controlled panic.

The wig is in the closet, buried beneath the pile of clothes on the floor where the rod fell four months ago. The care kit is shoved behind the mini-fridge. The RegenaForm receipt is deleted from my email and the browser history is cleared and the confirmation page is closed and every digital trace of the afternoon has been scrubbed with the thoroughness of a person who watches too many crime procedurals and not enough relationship advice videos.

The physical traces are harder to hide.

I stand in the bathroom. I look in the mirror. Not the phone camera — the actual mirror, the one I've been avoiding for eight months, the one that shows everything without filter or mercy.

The eyelashes are there. Long, dark, dense. They catch the light from the flickering overhead bulb and cast tiny shadows on my cheekbones that weren't there yesterday. The brows are reshaping — I can see it happening in real time, the slow migration from thick-masculine to feathered-feminine, the arch lifting by degrees, the hair refining itself from scraggly to sculpted. Dr. Maren said two to three hours. It's been ninety minutes. The transformation is halfway done.

Will she notice?

Of course she'll notice. Kira notices everything. She noticed the shampoo. She noticed when I changed my stream title two months ago. She noticed the brand of energy drink I switched to in September and asked if I was okay because "people only change energy drinks when something's wrong." She is an observation engine disguised as a girl with wire-frame glasses and she will look at my face and she will see the lashes and the brows and she will ask.

And I will lie.

The plan — "new skincare routine" — feels paper-thin, a tissue-paper excuse stretched over a truth-shaped hole. But it's what I have. And it might work, because the changes are subtle, because the lashes look natural, because the brows are only halfway to their final shape, because Kira has never paid close attention to my brow line (probably — I don't actually know what Kira has and hasn't paid attention to, given that she apparently memorized my 3 AM voice and my thumbnail-picking habit from video calls).

I splash water on my face. Pat dry. Look in the mirror one more time.

The face looking back is mine. Different, but mine. The lashes make my eyes look bigger. The partially reshaped brows make my forehead look smoother. The combined effect is — what did Dr. Maren say? — *lighter*. The face is lighter. More open. Not feminine, not yet, but softer in a way that's hard to pin down, like someone adjusted the contrast settings on a photograph.

My phone buzzes.

<<kira "leaving in 10! i'm bringing snacks. you better have cleared a space on that couch">>

<<eli "i don't have a couch">>

<<kira "your mattress. you better have cleared a space on your mattress. actually no we're watching at my place. your apartment makes me sad">>

<<eli "it makes me sad too">>

<<kira "then it's settled. my place. Packet misses you">>

<<eli "i met Packet once and he hissed at me">>

<<kira "that's his love language 😤">>

I smile. The muscles ache less today. They're adapting.

---

She picks me up at 6:07. Seven minutes late because she stopped to buy mochi from her mom's bakery ("Mom asked who I was bringing snacks for and I said 'a friend' and she gave me the look, Eli. THE LOOK. The Japanese-mother-who-knows-everything look.").

I get in the Civic. Cherry blossom. Heater. The warm, enclosed space that smells like her. She's wearing a cream cardigan over a black tank top, her hair freshly washed and slightly fluffier than usual, the bob bouncing at her jawline. The wire-frame glasses are slightly crooked — she pushes them up as I close the door.

<<kira "Hi.">>

<<eli "Hi.">>

She leans across the console. I lean to meet her. The kiss is quick — a hello kiss, a punctuation mark, a "this is a thing we do now" kiss — and her lips are warm and taste like the mochi she was sampling in the car. When she pulls back, she looks at my face.

She looks at my face for three seconds.

My heart rate spikes. Not the warm spike from yesterday's kiss — the cold spike, the adrenaline spike, the "she's going to see it" spike that sends electricity through my fingertips and makes my mouth go dry.

<<kira "Did you do something to your face?">>

There it is. The question. The one I prepared for, rehearsed, armed myself against. She asks it lightly — not accusatory, not suspicious, just curious, the tone of someone who notices a change in a face they've been studying for eighteen months.

<<eli "New skincare routine.">>

The lie exits my mouth with a smoothness that surprises me. Three words, evenly delivered, the vocal equivalent of a shrug. I even add a self-deprecating half-smile, the kind that says "I know, I know, I'm finally taking care of myself, don't make it a thing."

Kira squints. Her eyes narrow behind the glasses — not suspicion, not yet, but the focused attention of a person whose brain just flagged something without quite identifying it. She's looking at my lashes. I can feel her gaze on them, two points of heat on my eyelids.

<<kira "Hmm.">>

One syllable. Noncommittal. She doesn't push. Kira has this gift — the same gift Marcus has — of letting people keep their mysteries, of filing observations without demanding explanations. She heard my answer. She weighed it. She found it insufficient. And she chose to let it go.

For now.

<<kira "Well, it's working. You look good.">>

<<eli "Thanks.">>

<<kira "Like, actually good. Your eyes look... different. Brighter.">>

<<eli "It's the skincare routine.">>

<<kira "What products? I might need to try them.">>

<<eli "Uh. I don't remember the brand. It was at the drugstore.">>

<<kira "You bought skincare products at a drugstore and you don't remember the brand?">>

<<eli "I'm a guy, Kira. We just grab things.">>

She looks at me for one more second. Then she puts the car in drive and pulls away from the curb and the moment passes, and the splinter in my chest pushes a fraction of a millimeter deeper and I think: *that was close. That was the first test. There will be more.*

---

Kira's apartment is warm and lit and alive in the way my apartment is cold and dim and dying. Packet — the grey tabby, the keyboard sitter, the hisser — is on the couch when we walk in. He looks at me with the flat, appraising gaze of an animal that knows things humans don't, and I swear he can see through the eyelashes and the brows and the lie to the person underneath.

<<kira "Packet, be nice. Eli's our guest.">>

Packet does not be nice. Packet stares at me for four seconds, then turns his back and begins washing his paw with the elaborate disinterest of a creature that considers me beneath its notice. I respect this. Packet and I have an understanding: I don't expect affection and he doesn't provide any. It's the most honest relationship in this apartment.

<<kira "Tea?">>

<<eli "Sure.">>

She disappears into the kitchen. I hear the kettle fill, the burner click. I stand in the living room — the grey sectional, the coffee table with the manga and the orchid figurine now moved to a small shelf, the framed prints on the walls. Kira's apartment is curated the way her stream is curated — every object chosen, every placement intentional, the physical space reflecting the same aesthetic control she applies to her digital one. It's not obsessive. It's just... considered. The apartment of a person who thinks about where things go and why.

My apartment is the apartment of a person who dropped things and left them where they fell.

She returns with two mugs. Tea — green, the good kind, not the bag-in-a-cup kind. She hands me one and settles onto the couch, drawing her legs up beneath her, and pats the cushion beside her.

<<kira "Sit. I queued up an anime. Have you seen Frieren?">>

<<eli "No.">>

<<kira "Oh my GOD. Eli. You're going to love it. It's about this elf mage who outlives all her friends and then goes on a journey to understand human emotions and it's beautiful and devastating and the pacing is perfect and I cried four times.">>

<<eli "You're really selling the 'I cried four times' angle.">>

<<kira "Crying is a feature, not a bug.">>

I sit beside her. The couch cushions are firm and supportive — everything in Kira's life is firm and supportive, including the couch and the career and the girl herself — and I sink into them the way I sink into her company. Packet, having completed his paw-washing, relocates to the arm of the couch with the resigned tolerance of a cat who's accepted that the couch is no longer exclusively his territory.

Kira starts the show. The screen glows. The opening credits play — soft music, painted backgrounds, the kind of animation that looks like watercolors in motion. She tucks herself against my side with the natural, practiced motion of someone who's been waiting to do this, and I lift my arm and it goes around her shoulders and her head finds the hollow between my shoulder and my chest and she fits there like the space was designed for her.

Her hair is against my jaw. I can smell her shampoo — something floral, different from the cherry blossom of the car, more personal, the scent of her actual head on my actual shoulder. Her glasses press into my collarbone. She adjusts them without looking, one hand reaching up to tilt the frames so they're not digging in, and her fingers brush my neck in the process and the touch sends a ripple down my spine that I absorb without moving.

<<kira "Comfortable?">>

<<eli "Yeah.">>

<<kira "Good. No moving for the next three episodes.">>

<<eli "Three?">>

<<kira "Minimum. Frieren requires commitment.">>

We watch. The anime is — she's right, it's beautiful. A story about time and loss and the slow realization that the things you didn't pay attention to were the things that mattered most. An immortal elf looking back at a life she lived alongside mortals and understanding, too late, that the moments she dismissed as trivial were the only ones worth keeping.

I watch it and I feel things. Not the numb, flat nothing that usually occupies the space behind my sternum during screen time, but actual, genuine things — sadness and warmth and the ache of recognition, the specific ache of watching a character realize they wasted time and knowing you're doing the same thing. Kira shifts against me, adjusting her position, pressing closer. I hold her tighter. Not dramatically — just a fractional increase in the arm around her shoulders, a thumb moving slowly against the fabric of her cardigan. She makes a sound. A small "mmm" of contentment, barely audible, more vibration than voice.

The second episode ends. The third starts. Kira's commentary — which was frequent and passionate during the first episode ("LOOK at that background, Eli, LOOK at it") — has gotten quieter. Her voice is softer. Her observations are shorter. The gaps between them are longer.

Halfway through the third episode, she stops talking entirely.

I look down. Her eyes are closed. Her breathing has changed — slower, deeper, the rhythm of sleep. Her glasses have slipped down her nose. One hand is resting on my chest, the fingers loosely curled, the pixel-heart tattoo visible on her inner wrist. Her lips are slightly parted. There's a strand of hair across her cheek that the breathing moves, gently, back and forth, back and forth.

She's asleep.

Kira Nomura — 12,400 followers, 890 average viewers, $487 per session, bisexual, sharp-tongued, wire-framed, the most competent person I've ever met — is asleep on my shoulder at 8:45 PM on a Thursday in January, and I am going to sit here without moving for as long as she sleeps because waking her would be a crime.

I sit perfectly still. I breathe shallowly. The anime plays — the third episode continues, and I watch it over the top of her head, and the story unfolds, and the elf mage learns something about humans that she should have known before, and Kira sleeps against me with the trusting weight of a person who feels safe.

Packet, who has been observing from the arm of the couch, stands, stretches, and walks across the cushions to settle on Kira's lap. He curls into a circle, tucks his nose into his tail, and begins to purr. The vibration transfers through Kira's body into mine. Two living things, warm and sleeping and touching, and me — awake, holding them both, the stillest I've been in months.

I think about the eyelashes. The brows. The wig in my closet. The ElliePlayz dashboard with its $47 and its 51 followers. The RegenaForm receipt I deleted from my email. The lie I told thirty minutes ago in this car — "new skincare routine" — and the way Kira squinted and said "hmm" and let it go.

The splinter.

I can feel it now, deeper than before, positioned between the warmth of her body and the cold calculation of my afternoon. Two realities coexisting in the same chest: the real and the secret. The girl who fell asleep trusting me and the person I'm becoming without telling her. She's on my shoulder and I have her trust and I'm going to go home tonight and put on a wig and sit in front of a webcam and pretend to be a woman for money.

The anime ends. A fourth episode auto-plays. Kira sleeps through it. Packet purrs. I don't move.

I sit there for forty minutes.

She wakes at 9:30. The waking is slow — a stirring, a shift, a small confused sound as she orients herself. She pushes up from my shoulder, blinks, squints at the screen where a fifth episode has started.

<<kira "Oh my god. How long was I out?">>

<<eli "About forty-five minutes.">>

<<eli "You looked comfortable.">>

<<kira "Eli. You sat still for forty-five minutes?">>

<<eli "My arm is completely numb. I may never use it again.">>

She laughs. Sleepy, rough, the laugh of a person still halfway in a dream. She pushes her glasses up — they slipped almost to the tip of her nose during sleep — and runs a hand through her hair, which is flattened on the side that was against my shoulder, a lopsided bob that makes her look younger and softer and entirely devastating.

<<kira "You're ridiculous.">>

<<eli "You drooled on my shirt.">>

<<kira "I did NOT—">>

She looks at my shoulder. There's a small damp spot. Her face goes bright red.

<<kira "Oh my god. I'm so sorry.">>

<<eli "It's fine. I've had worse things on this shirt.">>

<<kira "That is NOT reassuring.">>

She swats my arm — the dead one, which I can't feel — and then leans in and kisses me. Not the hello kiss from the car. A longer one. A thank-you kiss. A "you sat still for forty-five minutes because I was sleeping" kiss. Her lips are dry from sleep and her breath is tea-warm and she tastes like green tea and mochi residue and the specific chemistry of a person who trusts you enough to fall asleep on your body.

<<kira "Thank you for not moving.">>

<<kira "Tomorrow?">>

The skin below the new lashes and above the new brows, in the space between the two modifications, the space that's still entirely Eli. Her thumb traces my cheekbone the way her thumb traced the condensation on the beer glass, the way her thumb traced circles on the back of my hand at the izakaya. A slow, deliberate circuit.

<<kira "You really do look different.">>

<<eli "Good different?">>

<<kira "Yeah. Good different. Your eyes are... I don't know. Bigger.">>

She's not buying it — I can see it in the way her brow furrows (her natural brow, the one that hasn't been sculpted by a clinic four blocks from my apartment), in the way her lips press together the way they do when she's choosing between pushing and letting go.

She lets go.

<<kira "Nothing. Just... you look nice. That's all.">>

<<eli "Thanks, Kira.">>

<<kira "Goodnight, disaster. ❤️">>

<<eli "Goodnight.">>

Then she pulls back and steps inside and the door closes and I stand in the hallway with her fingerprints on my face and her trust in my pocket and the lie between my teeth like a seed I can't swallow and can't spit out.

I get home at 10:15. The apartment. The crack. The hum.

I close the door. Stand in the dark. Don't turn on the light. The January night presses against the window and the building settles and the pipes clank and I stand there in my grey shirt with the drool spot on the shoulder and I feel the two realities pressing against each other inside my chest like tectonic plates.

The warm one: Kira. The couch. The sleeping. The trust. The kiss.

The cold one: ElliePlayz. The dashboard. The $47. The 89 viewers. The clinic. The wig in the closet.

Both real. Both mine. Both impossible to hold simultaneously without something cracking.

I turn on the light. The bulb flickers twice. Everything appears. The mattress, the desk, the monitors, the fridge, the burrito wrappers (two now, a growing collection). The closet with the wig buried in it.

I sit at the desk. Open the closet. Pull the wig out. Set it on the desk beside the monitors.

It sits there — brownish-auburn, soft waves, the StreamLine series from RegenaForm, designed for camera use — and it looks like a prop. A costume piece. A thing that belongs to someone else, waiting to be put on.

I open OBS. Open VoxMorph. Open the ElliePlayz channel. The dashboard from this afternoon's stream is still visible:

**Peak Viewers:** 89
**Average Viewers:** 63
**Followers:** 51
**Donations:** $47.00

51 followers. Some of them said they'd be back tonight. MidnightWhale99 said "tonight. I'll be there." ChillVibesOnly said "best new streamer on Twitch." People are waiting. Strangers who've never seen my face are waiting for a voice that doesn't exist to come back.

I put on the wig.

The process takes ten minutes — I watched a YouTube tutorial on the walk home, pausing at streetlights, memorizing the steps. Tuck the natural hair. Position the cap. Align the lace front. Secure with adhesive strips. The wig settles onto my head with a weight that's both physical and metaphorical — the feeling of putting on a costume, of stepping into a role, of becoming someone who isn't you and is entirely you.

I look at the webcam preview in OBS. The image is—

Different. Completely different.

The wig changes everything. The brownish-auburn waves frame the face in a way my greasy brown hair never did — soft, warm, feminine. The lashes catch the monitor light and cast those tiny shadows. The brows, now almost fully reshaped, lift the eye area into something open and inviting. The combination — wig, lashes, brows — doesn't make me look female. Not yet. The jaw is still too angular, the nose too wide, the skin too rough. But from certain angles, in certain light, with certain framing—

I adjust the webcam. Tilt it down. Frame the shot from the nose down. Chin, lips, jaw (the weakest part — the jaw gives it away), and the cascade of wig-hair falling over the shoulders. I angle my desk lamp to the side, creating a warm, directional light that softens the jaw shadow and makes the skin look smoother than it is.

In the preview, from the nose down, in this lighting, with this wig, I look like—

I look like a girl.

Not a beautiful girl. Not a convincing girl. But a plausible one. A girl who's shy about showing her face. A girl who streams with the camera low because she's nervous. A girl named Ellie who has a cute voice and 51 followers and whose full face reveal is always "coming eventually."

I turn on VoxMorph. Route the mic through OBS. Test the voice.

<<eli_as_ellie "Hey everyone, welcome back.">>

The voice comes through the monitoring feed — feminine, warm, the same voice from this afternoon — and combined with the visual of the wig and the chin and the lips in the webcam preview, it's... convincing. Not flawless. Not indistinguishable. But convincing the way a good magic trick is convincing — you know it's not real, but if you don't look too hard, the illusion holds.

I check the time. 11:00 PM. Late-night Twitch. The same time slot that worked this afternoon (which was actually early afternoon, different vibe, but the late-night slot is the one the guide recommended for new streamers).

I change the OBS scene. Add the webcam — the nose-down framing, the warm lighting. The ElliePlayz overlay, the pink-purple gradient, the cursive text. The game capture. Chat.

I hover over "Go Live."

My phone sits on the desk beside me. The last message on it is from Kira: a red heart emoji, sent as I left her apartment. The heart glows in the notification bar, a small red pulse, and I look at it and I look at the "Go Live" button and the distance between them is the distance between the person Kira thinks I am and the person I'm about to be.

I click it.

People are reacting to the face (the partial face, the suggestion of a face) the way they react to faces on Twitch: with attention, with projection, with the kind of parasocial intimacy that forms when a screen shows you just enough of a person to fill in the rest with your imagination.

My hands are shaking. Not from cold — the apartment is cold but my hands are shaking from something else, something that isn't temperature or fear or exhaustion. It's the tremor of a body processing numbers it wasn't built to process. $78.50. In ninety minutes. As Eli, that's five and a half months of donations. As Ellie, that's a Tuesday night.

I close OBS. Close VoxMorph. Take off the wig. Place it on the care form beside the desk. The brownish-auburn waves settle into stillness, and without the wig my face reappears in the dead webcam preview — the face beneath the costume, the person beneath the performance. Pale. Tired. Male. The new lashes look strange without the wig, like accessories from a different outfit, mismatched, out of context.

I check my phone. Two messages.

<<kira "hey, you still up? i can't sleep. keep thinking about you sitting still for 45 minutes so i could nap. that's like the most romantic thing that's ever happened to me and it's about immobility">>

<<kira "anyway goodnight for real this time. dream about something good again 🌙❤️">>

I read the messages. I read them twice. I feel the warmth — the Kira warmth, the real warmth, the warmth of a person who thinks about me at midnight and sends red hearts and calls my immobility romantic.

I feel the splinter.

Both at the same time. Both in the same chest. The warmth and the sharp point occupying the same space, coexisting the way two objects can't but two feelings can.

<<eli "goodnight kira. dreaming about something good. ❤️">>

I send the red heart. It costs me nothing to send it. That's the problem — it should cost something. It should feel earned or weighted or significant. But the heart emoji is free and the lie is free and the wig cost nothing and the lashes cost nothing and everything that's happening feels free until you look at the receipt and realize the currency isn't money.

It's trust. And I'm spending it.

I put the phone down. Lie on the mattress. Grey sheets. Thin comforter. Ceiling crack.

The numbers float in the dark behind my closed eyes:

EliPlays — Day 3: $3.50. Peak viewers: 5. Followers: 54.
ElliePlayz — Day 3: $125.50 (combined sessions). Peak viewers: 127. Followers: 119.

The gap. Except now the gap isn't between me and Kira. It's between me and myself. Between the person I am in the morning and the person I become at night. Between the stream nobody watches and the stream everybody wants. Between the red heart I send to a girl who trusts me and the secret I keep in a closet where the rod fell down four months ago.

I stare at the crack. It's growing. Everything is growing — the crack, the followers, the donations, the lie, the guilt. Everything is growing and nothing is stopping and the door that opened today opens only one way and I'm through it now, standing in a room I can't leave, wearing a face that isn't mine and isn't not-mine, holding a name that is two letters from my own.

Ellie.

Eli.

The same person. Different key.

I close my eyes. I sleep. And I don't dream about Kira and I don't dream about numbers.

I dream about a crack in a ceiling, splitting wider.
