I wake up with two things on my mind and one of them is the stranger's body.

The other is Kira's homework.

Both have been running in parallel since the moment I closed my eyes — the stranger occupying the lizard brain, the homework occupying the human one, and neither yielding territory. I'm lying on the grey sheets with the comforter tangled around one leg and the room is cold and the fridge hums and the ceiling crack is up there doing its geological thing and my body is announcing, with the blunt enthusiasm of a morning erection, that last night's session at midnight was insufficient to fully process the data the restaurant provided.

The stranger. The dark dress. The cheekbones in the red-gold light. The legs crossing, the fabric riding up an inch, the line my eyes followed from ankle to thigh. The breasts I couldn't name by letter but spent approximately ten minutes trying to classify using a catalog I read in a clinic waiting room. C cup. Maybe D. The question mark that became the rhythm that became the release that became the sleep that became this morning, where the question mark is back and the body wants to run the calculation again.

I don't. Not because of restraint — because the phone is buzzing.

9:22 AM. Three notifications.

Kira first. Even in the Other Arc — even in the version where I laughed off calling her hot and killed the moment with cowardice and she drove me home with a hand squeeze and homework — Kira first. Always Kira first. The gravity of her is constant even when the orbit is wrong.

<<kira "morning. did you sleep?">>

<<kira "also: the homework is not optional. actual words. actual feelings. whenever you're ready. no rush but also don't take six months.">>

Two messages. The first one checking on me — because she can't stop checking on me, because the caretaking is hardwired, because even after I stared at another woman over her pad thai she wakes up and asks if I slept. The second one reminding me the clock is running. No rush but also don't take six months. Kira's version of patience: infinite in theory, practical in execution.

I stare at the messages. The cursor blinks in the reply field. Actual words about actual feelings. She wants to know what I want. She wants me to figure it out and tell her and stop hiding behind "kidding" and thumbs-up emojis and the protective cowardice that has defined every emotional decision I've made for the past year and a half.

What do I want?

I want her. That part is simple, settled, has been settled since the first Discord call when her laugh did something to my circulatory system. I want Kira Nomura and her wire-frame glasses and her pixel-heart tattoo and her sharp bob and her "good boy" with the smirk emoji and her refusal to lie about moisturizer.

But I also want the $573 on the ElliePlayz dashboard. And the 252 followers. And the 339 peak viewers. And the nose appointment at RegenaForm. And the feeling of being seen — being watched, being valued, being paid to exist — that Ellie provides and Eli doesn't.

I want Kira and I want Ellie's life and the two wants exist in the same chest and one of them requires honesty and the other requires lies and I haven't figured out how to hold both without one crushing the other.

The homework. I'll do it. Today. After the nose. After the stream.

After the next thing. Always after the next thing.

I type a reply.

<<eli "i slept. working on the homework. give me today.">>

<<kira "today works. take the time you need. but eli — don't just tell me what you think i want to hear. tell me the real thing.">>

<<eli "i will">>

<<kira "okay ❤️">>

Red heart. She's still sending them. The consistency of her affection is its own kind of cruelty — not intentional, not malicious, just the quiet devastation of being loved by someone you're lying to.

I don't send one back. I send a period. A single dot. The smallest possible response that still registers as a response. She'll read it as hesitation. She'll be right.

Second notification. Marcus.

<<marc "yo. day off today. screenfixx is closed because the owner is 'conducting an internal review' which means devon finally broke something unfixable. want me to come thru later?">>

<<eli "yeah come by around 5? i have stuff this afternoon but evening works">>

<<marc "bet. i'll bring food. you still eating burritos?">>

<<eli "actually i was thinking i'd pick up food this time">>

<<marc "...you're buying food?">>

<<eli "is that so hard to believe">>

<<marc "eli. you haven't bought food since the obama administration">>

<<eli "things change">>

<<marc "apparently. what changed?">>

<<eli "the streaming stuff. i told you. numbers are better.">>

<<marc "the mysterious streaming stuff. one day you'll tell me what that actually means">>

<<eli "one day">>

<<marc "mhm. see you at 5. and if you're buying food, get something with vegetables. you have scurvy energy.">>

Marcus at 5. That gives me the morning for streaming, the early afternoon for RegenaForm, the late afternoon to prepare. The day has structure. Three acts: the stream, the nose, the friend. And underneath all three acts, running like a bass line, the homework.

Third notification. RegenaForm.

*Appointment Available: Tier 1 — Nose Refinement (Slope). Today, 2:00 PM. Suite 12. Book now?*

The app knows. It always knows. It tracked my earnings, calculated the timeline, identified the exact day I could afford the next modification, and pushed the notification with the precision of a dealer who knows your schedule.

I tap "Book."

*Confirmed: 2:00 PM. Dr. Voss. Suite 12. Estimated procedure time: 45 minutes.*

The nose. Today. By tonight — by 5 PM, when Marcus shows up with his direct eyes and his welfare scans — my nose will be different. Narrower. Straighter. Feminine where it was blunt. The bridge that sat at the center of my face like a declaration of masculinity, replaced by something that the algorithm would categorize differently. Something that earns its place in the frame.

I sit up. The grey sheets fall away. The apartment is the same — cold, dim, the crack, the fridge, the burrito wrappers I still haven't thrown away. But the person in it is becoming someone the apartment doesn't recognize. The apartment was designed for a man with $47 in the bank who ate frozen burritos and avoided mirrors. The person sitting on this mattress has $573 on a dashboard and a 2 PM appointment to reshape his face and a girl waiting for a text message that might change everything.

I get up.

Shower. Hot water — the eight-minute wait, now routine. The Marriott shampoo is gone. Used up. The hair gets bar soap — clean but lacking the hotel luster. I make a mental note: buy shampoo. Real shampoo. With money I earned. The novelty of planned purchases still hasn't worn off.

I brush my teeth. Mirror. The face — lashes, brows, the old nose for a few more hours. The upper face that reads feminine. The lower face that reads Eli. The nose sitting between them like an undecided voter, about to be swayed.

Black t-shirt today. Not the grey. The grey is Marcus's recommendation for impressing Kira and tonight is Marcus, not Kira, so the wardrobe reverts to the other option. Same joggers. Same hoodie. Same crushed sneakers.

9:55 AM. Stream first. Then nose. Then Marcus. Then homework.

The day is a checklist and I'm already running behind on the item that matters most.
