Brunch. Real brunch. Not a microwave burrito   actual food, ordered with actual money, delivered to my actual door by an actual human being on an actual bicycle.

I open a delivery app. The interface is familiar   I've scrolled through it before, hundreds of times, the way you scroll through a luxury car website when you ride the bus. Browsing as fantasy. Prices that existed in a currency I didn't have, menus that described a life I wasn't living.

Today the prices look different. Today they look like numbers instead of barriers.

I order from a place called Morning Glory   a breakfast spot six blocks away that I've walked past a hundred times and never entered. An egg sandwich with avocado and pickled onion on a brioche bun. A side of seasoned potatoes. A coffee   real coffee, not Monster Energy, not the cheap grounds I heat on the hot plate, but a latte. Oat milk. Large.

The total is $23.47 with delivery and tip.

Twenty-three dollars. That's roughly eleven minutes of streaming as Ellie, based on yesterday's per-hour rate. Eleven minutes of performing to fund one meal. The math makes the meal feel earned in a way that Marcus's bought coffees and Kira's treated ramen never did. Not because their generosity was wrong   because this is mine. My money. My meal. My eleven minutes of a manufactured voice turned into avocado and pickled onion and oat milk.

The food arrives twenty-two minutes later. A knock on the door. A paper bag. The delivery person doesn't look at my face   nobody ever looks at my face   and I take the bag and close the door and stand in my apartment holding the first meal I've bought for myself in longer than I can calculate.

I sit at the desk. Unwrap the sandwich. The brioche is golden and slightly warm and the avocado is ripe and the egg yolk is just runny enough to pool in the bottom of the wrapper when I bite and the pickled onion gives it a sharpness that cuts through the richness. The potatoes are crispy and seasoned with something herbal that I can't identify because my palate has been calibrated by eight months of microwave burritos and the subtlety of actual seasoning is a language I'm relearning.

The latte is good. Genuinely good. The oat milk froths into a texture that's smooth and sweet and I drink it slowly, sitting at my desk, looking at the morning's dashboard   $220, 300 followers   and the meal tastes like proof. Proof that the numbers translate. Proof that the voice and the name and the wig and the lashes convert into something tangible, something I can hold, something warm in my hands that isn't a Monster Energy can.

I eat everything. Every potato. Every drop of yolk. I drain the latte and set the cup beside the monitor and look at it   the empty paper cup with the Morning Glory logo, a sun rising over a coffee bean   and I feel full. Not just physically. Full in the way that a person feels full when they've done something for themselves without asking permission or assistance or apologizing for the cost.

$23.47 well spent. The best eleven minutes of streaming I've ever invested.

12:45 PM. Appointment at 2:00. Time to get ready.

I shower again   bar soap this time, no shampoo, but the hair is clean enough from this morning. Grey shirt. Joggers. Hoodie. Sneakers. The uniform. But the person wearing it has a latte in his bloodstream instead of Monster Energy and the difference is chemical and philosophical and visible in the way I stand, slightly straighter, slightly less like a question mark with shoes.

They tap the wall screen. My face appears again   high resolution, merciless, the clinical lighting finding every flaw. But the face looks different than it did two days ago. The lashes frame the eyes. The brows lift the forehead. The modifications I've already made create a foundation that makes the remaining masculine features   nose, jaw, lips   stand out more by contrast. The face is a renovation half-complete, the beautiful new paint on the upper walls making the scuffed floors more visible.

The digital overlay activates. The nose morphs   the bridge narrowing, the tip refining, the asymmetry correcting. The change is subtle and seismic. The face on screen with the modified nose looks not just different but resolved   as if the nose was the missing puzzle piece, the bridge between the feminized upper face and the still-masculine lower face. With the nose done, the crop line can drop. The webcam can show eyes plus nose. The viewers will see a face that's two-thirds feminine and the remaining third   jaw, lips   will be the next project.

<<eli "Yeah. Let's go.">>

They apply the compound. Cool gel on the bridge of my nose, across the tip, along the sides where the nostrils meet the cheek. Then warmth   deeper than the lashes, deeper than the brows. The nose has more structure, more cartilage, more architecture to reshape. The warmth penetrates into the bone, into the bridge, into the places where the face's infrastructure lives.

I close my eyes. Forty minutes.

I think about Kira. About tonight. About the gyudon and the apartment and the truth I'm going to tell her. I rehearse the words in my head the way she rehearsed her confession in the shower   practicing, refining, trying to find the sequence that explains without excusing, that confesses without begging, that tells the truth without losing the person I'm telling it to.

Too ominous. She'd panic.

*Kira, you know how you said the system can be hacked?*

Better. Starts with her. Starts with the shared knowledge.

*I found a way. The voice modulator. A new account. A different name. And it worked   Kira, it worked so fast it scared me. And then I came here   to this clinic   and they changed my face. Not all of it. The lashes and the brows you noticed. And now the nose. Today. Right now, while you're buying groceries for gyudon, someone is reshaping my nose four blocks from my apartment because the streaming economy pays for a face I'm building instead of the one I was born with.*

Too long. Too much at once. She'd overload.

*Kira, I'm Ellie.*

Three words. Nuclear. But accurate.

The warmth continues. I feel the reshaping   not pain, not exactly, but the awareness of change. The cartilage shifting. The bridge narrowing millimeter by millimeter. The tip losing its roundness, gaining an angle, softening from something that reads as male into something that reads as a question the viewer answers with "female" because the lashes and brows and hair have already primed the answer.

Lina leads me to the fitting room. The lit vanity mirror. The wig forms on the wall. She selects my StreamLine wig   the brownish-auburn waves, the one I've been wearing for three days, the one that lives on a care form in my closet beneath the pile of clothes where the rod fell.

She puts it on me. The practiced routine   tuck, position, align, secure. Her vanilla-and-linen hands adjusting the lace front with impersonal precision.

<<lina "There.">>

I look in the mirror.

The person looking back at me is not Eli. The person looking back at me is not quite Ellie either   not the full Ellie, not the face reveal Ellie, not the version the viewers are building in their imaginations from a pair of eyes and a voice. But the person in the mirror is closer to Ellie than Eli has ever been.

The wig frames the face. The lashes anchor the eyes. The brows arch softly. And now the nose   the Slope nose, narrow and straight and gently angled   sits at the center of the composition like a keystone, the element that connects the feminized eyes to the still-masculine mouth and makes the upper face read as coherent. Complete. A girl's eyes above a girl's nose above a jawline that still needs work but can be hidden, cropped, managed.

From the eyes to the nose. The crop line drops. The webcam can show more. Tonight, the viewers will see Ellie's eyes AND Ellie's nose and the face that emerges will be real   not AI, not a filter, not a projection. Real lashes, real brows, real nose, real skin. The lie built entirely from truth.

I stare for ten seconds. Fifteen. Lina waits, patient, the smile holding.

<<lina "The refinement suits you. The proportions are harmonizing beautifully.">>

<<eli "Thank you.">>

<<lina "Dr. Voss wanted me to mention   the next logical step in the Tier 1 progression would be lip enhancement. Natural-Full. Four hundred Dollars. But there's no rush. The nose alone will make a significant difference to your framing.">>

The upsell. Always the upsell. The next step mentioned casually, a seed planted in the fertile soil of visible progress. But I barely hear it. I'm looking at the mirror and the mirror is showing me a version of myself that I   I don't know what I feel. The feeling doesn't have a name. It's not pride, not exactly. Not disgust, not exactly. It's something in between, something new, the specific emotion of watching your own face become someone else's and recognizing both versions as yourself.

I take off the wig. Hand it to Lina. The face returns to Eli   but a different Eli than five days ago. An Eli with long lashes and arched brows and a straight, narrow nose that doesn't match the jawline or the lips but does match the direction. The direction is feminine. The direction is Ellie. The direction is money.

<<eli "Thanks, Lina. I'll think about the lips.">>

<<lina "Of course. We're always here.">>

She says "always" the same way MidnightWhale99 says "always." The certainty of a system that knows you'll return because the loop requires it.

I leave the clinic. The January air hits the new nose   the cold felt differently on the narrowed bridge, the sensation of air moving through refined nostrils, the physical novelty of a feature I've had my entire life suddenly being different. I touch it, gently, the way you touch something expensive. The bridge is smooth. Straight. Narrow. The tip is softer than the original, gently angled instead of rounded. It feels like someone else's nose on my face.

It feels like Ellie's nose on Eli's face.

Four blocks home. I walk with the hood up, not because I'm hiding but because the nose needs to settle and the cold isn't helping. In my pocket, the phone buzzes.
