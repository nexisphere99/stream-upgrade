10:47 PM. The apartment. The desk. The ritual.

Except the ritual is different tonight.

I stand in the bathroom and look in the mirror   the real mirror, the one I avoided for eight months and have been using daily for five. The face looking back has three modifications. Lashes: long, dark, dense. Brows: feathered, arched, feminine. Nose: Slope. Narrow bridge. Straight profile. Soft tip. The upper two-thirds of the face that reads as girl.

The lower third   jaw, lips, chin   is still Eli. Still unchanged. Still the sketch beneath the finished painting.

But tonight, for the first time, the webcam is going to show the nose.

I sit at the desk. Wig on   three minutes flat, the muscle memory hardened into autopilot. VoxMorph calibrated. OBS open. The webcam framing   this is the change. I tilt the camera down. Not much. An inch. Maybe an inch and a half. Enough to bring the nose into frame.

Eyes. Lashes. Brows. Nose. Hair.

The crop line drops. The preview shows more face than any stream before. The nose sits in the frame like a bridge between the eyes the viewers know and the lips they don't, a new feature anchoring the composition, connecting the feminized upper face to the mystery below.

I angle the desk lamp. The warm gold catches the bridge of the new nose   the straight, narrow bridge, the subtle highlight that Dr. Maren's refinement created. The slope catches light differently than the original   where the old nose absorbed light bluntly, the new one channels it, the planes of the refined cartilage creating shadows and highlights that photograph like an editorial.

I check the OBS preview one more time. The face in the rectangle is   more. More than the eyes-only streams. More than the suggestion. The nose gives the face dimensionality, turns the flat mask of eyes-and-hair into something three-dimensional, something that approaches a person rather than a crop of one.

She looks real.

Ellie looks real.

The numbers float behind my closed eyes. $472 today. $1,196 total. 370 followers. 347 peak. The nose fund. The cathedral metaphor. The wink with the nose in frame.

But the numbers share the ceiling tonight with other images. Better images.

Kira's face when she saw the flowers. The softening. The widening. The arms around me with the brown paper crinkling between our chests.

Her hand on my face. Tracing the nose. The bridge, the slope, the tip. Learning the new geography with the careful attention of a cartographer mapping undiscovered territory.

The kiss. The taste of soy sauce and trust.

Her hand. Moving down. The waistband. The question   "is this okay?"   asked with her fingers on the elastic and her eyes on mine and the answer in my voice before the word formed.

I think about her hand. The small, warm grip. The pixel-heart tattoo disappearing beneath the fabric. The pressure and the rhythm and the way she adjusted   reading me, the way she reads chat, the way she reads everything, with attention and care and the specific intelligence of a person who wants to get it right.

My hand moves. Under the comforter. Under the waistband. Not toward her   toward the memory of her. The phantom grip. The echo of a touch that happened three hours ago on a couch four blocks away.

I close my eyes. I'm not imagining a fantasy this time. I'm replaying reality. The actual memory. The feel of her hand. The warmth. The "mmnh" she made against my temple. The way she said "let me" like it was a gift she was giving and receiving simultaneously.

The pressure against my palm. The specific curl of her fingers. Small over large. The pixel-heart tattoo that I couldn't see but knew was there, pressed against me in the dark beneath the elastic, an invisible tattoo touching an invisible place, two hidden things meeting in the space between fabric and skin.

Her voice. "How do you feel?" Asked afterward, gentle, the analytical engine softened into something purely warm. Not assessing. Not processing. Just asking. Just a girl who wanted to know if she'd made someone happy.

I come. Quieter than last time alone, but deeper   the release carrying less tension because there's less tension to carry. The splinter is smaller. The secret is shared. The hand that touched me tonight was real and the person attached to it knows the truth and stayed and called me boyfriend for the first time.

I clean up. Tissue. Pull the comforter to my chin.

The ceiling crack is up there. Still growing. But the growth is slower in the dark, or maybe I'm slower to notice it, or maybe the ceiling has other things to report tonight   the $1,196, the 370 followers, the flowers in a water glass in an apartment four blocks away, the taste of gyudon, the sound she made when she saw the sweet peas.

I think about tomorrow. The morning stream. Ellie with the nose. The numbers climbing. The nose fund viewers celebrating their investment. The cathedral, stone by stone.

I think about Kira. The deal we made. No more lies. No modifications I don't want. Only changes for me. The promise sitting alongside the truth, the two of them sharing a shelf that's finally large enough for both.

I think about the latte this morning. The first meal I bought myself. The oat milk. The brioche. The $23.47 that tasted like earning instead of surviving.

Sleep comes easier tonight. Not because the problems are solved   the jaw is still masculine, the lips are still thin, the voice is still software, the wig is still necessary, the loop is still running. The problems are all still here. But the person carrying them isn't carrying them alone anymore.

Kira knows. Kira stays. Kira said "boyfriend."

That's enough. For tonight. That's enough.

I sleep and I dream about a girl with wire-frame glasses tracing a nose that wasn't there yesterday, saying "it suits you" in a voice that sounds like home, in an apartment that smells like soy sauce and sweet peas and the specific warmth of a person who chose to stay.
