### DAY 23 — "The Studio / First Session"

Eli sits. The chair in front of the seamless white backdrop. The softbox lights hum — warm, even, professional. A quality of light that's completely different from the desk lamp improvisation of the apartment. Under these lights, the face is revealed without shadows to hide behind, without the crop line's editorial protection. Just the face. Full. Exposed.

Sophie circles behind the camera. Adjusts the lens. Her movements are precise — the economy of a person who has done this thousands of times and for whom the camera is an extension of the hand rather than a tool held by one.

<<sophie "Look at the lens.">>

Eli looks. The lens is a black circle, a pupil without an iris, the eye of a machine operated by a person whose grey-green eyes are just visible above the camera body.

<<sophie "Don't perform. You're performing. I can see the streamer switch — the lean-in, the eye contact, the brightness. Turn it off.">>

<<eli "I don't know how to turn it off. It's been on for weeks.">>

<<sophie "Then look through the lens instead of at it. Don't see the audience. See the wall behind me. Let your face go slack. Let it be what it is without asking it to work.">>

He tries. The performative muscles — the ones he's been training for weeks, the wide-eyes, the gasp, the cute concentration face — slowly release. The face goes neutral. The face goes Eli. Not Ellie-performing, not Eli-hiding. Something between. The face at rest.

Sophie's shutter clicks. Once. Twice. She moves — changes angle, kneels, stands on a step stool, shoots from above. The clicks are rhythmic, unhurried. She doesn't give directions between shots. She watches through the viewfinder and waits and the waiting is its own instruction: be still. Be seen. Let the camera find you instead of you finding the camera.

Twenty minutes. Forty shots. She lowers the camera.

<<sophie "Come look.">>

The laptop. The photos loading. RAW files, unprocessed, the face at its most naked — no filter, no compression, no Twitch encoding to soften the edges. Just the face, in light, as it is.

Eli looks at the photos and something happens to his chest. The person in the images is — not Ellie-the-streamer. Not Eli-the-failure. Someone else. Someone whose face is beautiful and tired and uncertain and the uncertainty is visible in the eyes the way weather is visible in a sky. The construction is there — the sculpted brows, the refined nose, the porcelain skin — but beneath the construction, looking through it the way light looks through stained glass, there's a person. A real, unperformed person who exists underneath the project.

<<sophie "There it is.">>

<<eli "There what is?">>

<<sophie "The person under the project. That's what I wanted to photograph.">>

The sentence breaks something. Not dramatically — the breaking is quiet, structural, the way a wall cracks before it gives way. He's been seen. Not by an audience, not through a screen, not at a distance. Seen by a person, in a room, through a lens that was looking for exactly the thing he's been hiding.

<<eli "Nobody's ever... nobody's looked at me like that.">>

<<sophie "Like what?">>

<<eli "Like the construction is the interesting part and not the product.">>

<<sophie "That's because most people consume images. I deconstruct them. You've built something remarkable. Most people who modify their faces are trying to erase themselves. You're trying to become something specific. The specificity is what makes it art.">>

<<eli "It's not art. It's a business strategy.">>

<<sophie "Those aren't mutually exclusive.">>

She sends him the files. All forty. Unedited. "Edit them yourself if you want. Or don't. The raw versions are better."

He leaves the studio at 4 PM. Walks home. The January air on the face that was just photographed by a person who called the construction art. The photos sit on his phone like a weight — not the weight of evidence (the way the ElliePlayz dashboard feels) but the weight of being witnessed.

He looks at the photos on the mattress that night. In the dark. The face in professional light, every modification visible, every seam between Eli and Ellie documented with the precision of someone who sees seams the way a jeweler sees facets. The photos are beautiful. Not because the face is beautiful (it is). Because the looking is beautiful. Because the photographer's attention is in the image, embedded in the light and the angle and the moment the shutter chose to click.

He texts Sophie at midnight.

<<eli "the photos are incredible">>

<<sophie "the subject was interesting. same time next week? i want to document the progression.">>

<<eli "the progression of what?">>

<<sophie "of you becoming whoever you're becoming. i think it's worth documenting.">>

<<eli "okay. next week.">>

### DAY 25 — "Second Session / The Waist in Light"

Sophie's studio. The softbox lights. The seamless backdrop. But today the setup is different — she's arranged a standing frame instead of the chair. The full body is the subject now.

<<sophie "shirt off.">>

<<eli "...what?">>

<<sophie "the waist. i need to photograph the waist. shirt off.">>

He hesitates. He's been shirtless on stream (the crop top, the fitted tank, the carefully framed shots). But on stream there's a screen. Here there's a person. Three feet away. With eyes that see construction instead of skin.

He takes the shirt off. The apartment cold is absent here — Sophie's studio is warm, the lights contributing heat. He stands in the standing frame in jeans and nothing else, the upper body exposed: narrow shoulders, smooth swan neck, flat chest (male, unmodified), hourglass waist, luminous porcelain skin. The body of a person who is female everywhere except the territory between collarbone and navel, where the flat male chest is the last holdout.

Sophie photographs. Circles. The shutter clicking in that unhurried rhythm. She photographs the waist from five angles — front, three-quarter, side, back, above. She photographs the transition points — where the feminine shoulders meet the flat chest, where the flat chest meets the feminine waist. The contradictions.

<<sophie "the contradictions are the most interesting part.">>

<<eli "most people would call them problems.">>

<<sophie "most people see a body as a finished product. i see it as a work in progress. works in progress are always more interesting than finished pieces.">>

She shows him the photos. The waist in light. The contradiction documented — the feminine frame, the masculine gap, the body in two languages simultaneously. The photos are arresting. Not beautiful in the conventional sense (the flat chest disrupts the composition). Beautiful in the way unfinished buildings are beautiful — the skeleton visible, the intention clear, the completion implied by the incompletion.

After the session, she makes tea. A small kitchen area in the corner of the studio — a kettle, two mugs, loose-leaf green tea. The domesticity is minimal, functional, stripped of decoration. Like Sophie herself.

They sit on the studio floor. Concrete, cold, cushioned by folded backdrop paper. Tea in hand. The intimacy of two people sitting on a floor in a space that belongs to one of them.

<<sophie "tell me about the clinic.">>

<<eli "regenaform. four blocks from my apartment.">>

<<sophie "i know regenaform.">>

<<eli "you do?">>

<<sophie "i've photographed three of their clients. transition documentation. before, during, after. they send me referrals occasionally.">>

<<eli "dr. maren sent you?">>

<<sophie "maren mentioned a new client with an interesting progression. they didn't give me your name. they showed me your face — the 'before' scan from your first consultation. i recognized you from the stream. the rest was observation.">>

The architecture of it clicks into place. RegenaForm and Sophie are connected. The clinic that builds faces and the photographer who documents them — two branches of the same industry. Dr. Maren referred Eli to Sophie without telling either of them directly. The door that "only opens one way" has another door behind it, and behind that door is a woman with a camera who's been watching from the beginning.

<<eli "so this isn't random.">>

<<sophie "nothing about this is random. you found regenaform because the algorithm showed you the ad. i found your stream because maren mentioned the case. you found me in chat because i commented. the system connects the nodes.">>

<<eli "that sounds paranoid.">>

<<sophie "it sounds like marketing. which is what it is. but the work — the photos, the documentation, you becoming whoever you're becoming — that part is real. that part is mine.">>

<<eli "yours?">>

<<sophie "i mean it's my interest. not my property. your body, your face, your project. i just want to point a camera at it.">>

The "just" does heavy lifting. Sophie's interest is professional — the photographer documenting the subject. But the quality of her attention, the warmth of the tea, the fact of sitting on a floor in a studio at 4 PM on a Thursday — these are personal. The professional is the entry point. What grows from it is something else.

### DAYS 26–28 — "Height, Skin, Hips / Weekly Sessions"

### DAY 29 — "The Portfolio / First Touch"

Sophie has compiled a portfolio. Twelve sessions' worth of photographs (she's been counting the sessions differently — including the initial chat observations as pre-sessions). The portfolio is organized chronologically: the face with only lashes and brows, the nose addition, the full face, the throat, the hands, the waist, the hips. A visual timeline of the transformation.

She shows it to Eli on the studio laptop. They sit side by side at the table — the closest they've been physically. Sophie's shoulder against Eli's shoulder. Her arm against his arm. The contact is incidental, practical — two people looking at a screen need to be close enough to see it.

But the contact registers. Eli's skin, modified to porcelain-smooth and sensitized, feels Sophie's arm through the sleeve of her grey linen shirt. The warmth of another person's body, a warmth he receives from Marcus weekly (the shoulder-to-shoulder Rift Walkers position) but rarely from anyone else. The studio is warm. Sophie is warm. The proximity is professional and personal simultaneously, the two states existing in the same body the way Eli and Ellie exist in the same body.

<<sophie "the lash session — this is where it started visually. the eyes changed the read of the entire face.">>

Her finger on the screen, pointing. Then her finger moves from the screen to his face — tracing the lash line. The gesture is clinical, a photographer referencing her subject, but the touch is not clinical. The touch is warm and specific and her fingertip moves along the ridge of engineered lashes the way you'd trace a brushstroke in a painting you're studying.

<<eli "sophie.">>

<<sophie "sorry. occupational hazard. i touch the things i photograph.">>

<<eli "i didn't say stop.">>

Sophie's finger pauses. On his cheekbone. The Soft & Full cheekbone, the modification that added volume and caught light. Her fingertip rests there for three seconds. Then she withdraws.

<<sophie "the portfolio. i want to publish it. not your real name — 'anonymous subject, ongoing transition documentation.' a gallery show. small, invitation-only. the work is strong enough.">>

<<eli "you want to put my face in a gallery?">>

<<sophie "i want to put the PROCESS in a gallery. the face is the evidence. the process is the art.">>

He doesn't answer immediately. The idea of his face — Ellie's face, the constructed face — hanging on a gallery wall is simultaneously thrilling and terrifying. Thrilling because someone sees the construction as art. Terrifying because art is permanent, visible, examinable by anyone who walks into the room.

<<eli "let me think about it.">>

<<sophie "take your time.">>

- **Sophie Status:** First intentional touch. The finger on the cheekbone. Three seconds. The professional and personal crossing like wires.
