I wake up and the first thing I do is touch my cheeks.

Not the mirror. Not the phone. The cheeks. Both hands, fingers spread, pressing against the cheekbones that were restructured yesterday afternoon. The bone is there — rounded, warm, settled. The duality that's become the baseline emotional frequency of my life — the persistent, low-grade dissonance of a face that belongs to me because it was made from me but doesn't belong to the me I've been for twenty-two years.

I drop my hands. Open my eyes. The ceiling crack. Growing. Always growing. The plaster splitting along a line that runs from above the window to somewhere behind the light fixture, a geological timeline of neglect measured in millimeters per month. My landlord's never going to fix it. My landlord doesn't know my face is changing. My landlord doesn't know anything about me except that rent arrives three days late every month via an automated transfer from a bank account that, until six days ago, contained less than a quality meal.

Thirty-six hours. That's how long it's been since our last exchange. The red heart at 9:47 PM two nights ago. Thirty-six hours of nothing. Thirty-six hours of a thread sitting warm and open like a door that both of us are standing in front of and neither of us is walking through. Except it's not "neither" — it's me. She walked through. I need to text her. Not "need" in the way I need to stream or need to visit RegenaForm — those are operational needs, the needs of the loop, the machine requirements. I need to text Kira in the way a person needs to breathe after holding their breath — the need that builds until it becomes the only thing, the need that punishes avoidance with increasing pressure. Every hour I don't text her, the text becomes harder. Every hour, the opening message requires more words, more honesty, more explanation for the gap. At twenty-four hours, "hey" might have worked. At thirty-six hours, "hey" is an insult.

I put the phone down. I'll text her after the morning stream. I'll text her when I have the morning numbers as a warm cushion beneath the anxiety, when the dopamine from the viewer count and the donation total has softened the edges of the courage I need.

After the stream. Always after.
